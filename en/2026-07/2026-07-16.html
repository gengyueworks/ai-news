07-16 · Thinking Machines · Open Weights
Inkling becomes the new U.S. open-source benchmark with an AI Index of 41, though not yet the world's strongest

Mira Murati's Thinking Machines releases its first fully open-source model Inkling:9750 B parameters, active per token 410 BMixture-of-Experts architecture, Apache 2.0 license, 45 trillion-token multimodal pre-training, longest context for weighted version 100 M tokens.Third-party evaluation agency Artificial Analysis added it to their intelligence index, scoring 41, becomingthe top rank in the U.S. open-source weight camp, outperforming NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra 's 38, Gemma 4 31B 's 29, and gpt-oss-120b's 24.

Primary intelligence visual archive

Yet it is not today's strongest model. In the same index, it still trails China's open-source camp GLM-5.2 and Kimi K2.6, and cannot catch up to top-tier proprietary models.It is TML's "first cornerstone," not the "grand finale flagship".

Two data points are worth noting. First, efficiency: Artificial Analysis measured that Inkling outputs an average of only 2.5 M output tokens per intelligence index task, while GLM-5.2 max requires 4.3 M, and Kimi K2.6 requires 3.8 M. Second, pricing: on Tinker, 64K context input costs per million tokens 1.87 USD, and output costs 4.68 USD. The same event also previewed Inkling-Small,2760 B total parameters,120 B active.

The ecosystem expanded on launch day: vLLM reported reaching up to 380 tokens/sec/user on 4×GB200, and NVIDIA confirmed it was trained using GB300 NVL72.When cutting-edge labs start handing over weights and admitting "I am not the strongest," another layer of the barrier between proprietary and open source is pierced.

07-16 · Poolside · Open Source Coding Models
Poolside Open-Sources Laguna, 33B for Local Execution, 225B for Long-Range Tasks

Poolside has released two open-weight agentic coding models, Laguna XS 2.1 and M.1: XS 2.1 is 33B an MoE (activating 3Btokens per token) small enough to run locally on a single desktop GPU, while M.1 is 225B(activating 23B) flagship designed for long-range software engineering.The weights are released under Apache/OpenMDW licenses and integrate into local inference stacks like Ollama and vLLM.

It clearly divides "coding models" into two tiers:lightweight for the edge, heavy for the cloud, allowing enterprises to choose as needed..
