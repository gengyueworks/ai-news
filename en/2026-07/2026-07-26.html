Claude Opus 5 Released: Performance Nears Fable 5 at Half the Price, But the Real Hard Battle Has Just Begun

Claude Opus 5 is released.

Primary intelligence field evidence and architecture visual

On July 25, Anthropic released Claude Opus 5. The company stated that its intelligence level approaches its own Fable 5, but its API pricing is only half of the latter: per million input tokens 5 USD, output 25 USD, on par with Opus 4.8.It instantly became the default model for Claude Max and topped programming and knowledge work benchmarks such as Frontier-Bench and GDPval-AA.

On the same day, AWS announced the integration of Opus 5 into Bedrock, and TrendAI also incorporated it into vulnerability prioritization assessment.When even the most expensive flagship starts chasing "cost-effectiveness," the main axis of competition for frontier models has shifted from "who is stronger" to "who is cheaper yet usable."

Joint Petition by Three Giants: Do Not Embargo Open-Source Weights, But the Real Hard Battle Has Just Begun

The open-source weight debate sparked by Kimi K3 continues to heat up. Led by NVIDIA, Microsoft, and Meta, a coalition of 20 companies issued a joint letter warning policymakers against imposing embargoes or restrictions on open-source weights, including Chinese models.The letter states that open models that can be downloaded, modified, and self-deployed are key to maintaining U.S. technological leadership.

Primary intelligence field evidence and architecture visual

Notably, neither OpenAI nor Anthropic signed the letter, as both primarily advocate for closed-source models.With closed-source giants preparing for IPOs on one side and hardware makers and the open-source camp defending their ground on the other, the first public game of AI governance has been laid on the table.

July · Figure / BMW · Physical AI

Figure 03 Enters BMW Production Line: Robots Move from Showroom to Logistics Line

The next-generation Figure 03 has been deployed to Plant 52 at BMW's Spartanburg facility in the US, taking on logistics sorting: picking out out-of-order parts and sequencing them for the production line. The initial batch of 40 units is planned by BMW to expand to 200 units; based on RaaS (Robot-as-a-Service) leasing 25 per hour, lower than local workers' hourly wages.

Primary intelligence field evidence and architecture visual

The previous-generation Figure 02 worked in the body shop for 11 months and participated in the manufacturing of 30,000 X3s; this new generation has been moved to more tedious logistics operations. What robots take on first are the jobs humans least want to do.

Opus 5 Writes Its Own CV Pipeline: Reconstructing 3D Models from Raw Pixels

Opus 5 Writes Its Own CV Pipeline.

Primary intelligence field evidence and architecture visual

In a test, a developer gave Opus 5 an image of a mechanical part without letting it view the image directly. It wrote its own computer vision pipeline, extracted geometry from raw pixels, and ultimately reconstructed a 3D model using FreeCAD. Another user asked it to make a presentation, and it simply wrote a slide rendering engine from scratch.

Models are beginning to build their own tools rather than just using them. The wall between creators and engineers is quietly being leveled by such "just casually build one yourself" moments.

Sources: Malpass

June · NVIDIA · Computing Power

Vera Rubin Enters Full Production: The Computing Infrastructure Remade for "Agents"

NVIDIA has confirmed that Vera Rubin NVL72 has entered full production: a single GPU integrates 288 GB HBM4 with an aggregate bandwidth of 22 TB/s. Tailored for the agent era, it can pack 40% more GPUs under the same power consumption, with inference energy efficiency roughly 10 times that of Blackwell.

In the second half of the computing power competition, it is no longer about "who has more GPUs," but "how many useful tokens you can output per unit of electricity."

July · Big Five · Financials

Hidden 1.65 Trillions in Off-Balance-Sheet Debt

According to a Nikkei investigation, five tech giants—Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta, and Oracle—have tucked away approximately 1.65 trillion dollars in off-balance-sheet debt, mostly tied to data centers and compute leases, exceeding what is disclosed in their quarterly reports by 3000 hundred million.

AI's appetite for compute is turning "borrowing to build data centers" into an increasingly sophisticated financial engineering feat; if these debts seep into pension and life insurance portfolios, it spells systemic risk.
