Reporter Inside a Robotics Factory: Are Humanoid Robots Really Ready for Your Home?

Neo is priced at $20,000 to buy out or $499 per month to rent. But the real headline isn't the price—early home users must still allow human employees from 1X to remotely operate the robots in their homes. Image: The New Yorker.

Primary intelligence field evidence and architecture visual

The New Yorker Reporter Stephen Witt Visits 1X Located in Hayward, California, spanning approximately 5.8 10,000 sq ft, with over200 employees, now in mass production. The goal is to bring Neo into American homes starting by the end of 2026.

Standing about 168cm, weighing around 30kg, focused on chores and companionship, priced at $20,000 to buy out or $499/month to rent. 1X states annual production capacity will reach 10 units by the end of 2027, with another San Carlos factory operational this year.

However, Witt's report uncovers the industry's least publicized side:so-called "autonomy" is currently more of a vision.Early users in 2026 will still need to allow human 1X employees to remotely control the robots at home. One researcher describes the current capability boundary:"A robot that can do a backflip may not necessarily be able to walk up a flight of stairs."

As "robots entering the home" shifts from concept to order, the real threshold isn't mobility, but trust: whether you are willing to let a semi-autonomous, semi-teleoperated machine live in your living room.

8 Behind the Billion-Dollar Funding: The Cloud Powering "Open-Source AI" Doubles Its Valuation in a Year

The AI cloud built for open-source models Together AI completed its 8 Series C round on July 1, with a post-money valuation of 83 billion, more than doubling from 33 billion in February 2025. This round was led by Saudi Aramco's Aramco Ventures, with participation from NVIDIA, General Catalyst, Vista, and others.

The company stated that its annualized bookings last quarter exceeded 11.5 billion. The lead investor from energy capital shows that Middle Eastern sovereign funds are accelerating inflows into the AI infrastructure "pick-and-shovel" race. Positioned as a cloud for running open-source weight models, it directly contrasts with the closed-source API-only route,and it's not just labs betting on open source—it's also the cloud powering them.

Capital is betting that "open-source models must also run at scale." The frontier no longer belongs solely to giants building their own supercomputers; those who can rent compute can also join the table.

Same Pricing, Quietly More Expensive: The Invisible Price Hike of a Tokenizer

"What's new in Claude Sonnet 5"—Simon Willison's tests reveal the new tokenizer generates about 30% more tokens for the same text. Image: simonwillison.net.

Primary intelligence field evidence and architecture visual

Anthropic launched in late June Claude Sonnet 5, with official pricing matching the previous generation. However, independent developer Simon Willison found:The new tokenizer generates about 30% more tokens for the same text.

Not a single digit changed on the price list, but the bills quietly went up. The new model context 100 10k tokens, max output 12.8 10k, with adaptive thinking enabled by default. But the increased token consumption for equivalent text equals an unannounced price hike.

The true cost of models isn't on the price list; it's hidden in "how many tokens a sentence costs." The invisible tokenizer is the real price switch.

FLARE-AI / Hugging Face · Jul 4
FLARE-AI Launches: A Unified "Incident Reporting" Channel for AI Failures

FLARE-AI Initiated by a coalition of cybersecurity and AI safety researchers, with a specific goal: standardize the reporting of AI system incidents and flaws, routing issues to corresponding developers and vulnerability databases,rather than vanishing into isolated feedback forms. Co-backed by the Hugging Face CEO and multiple safety researchers.

Behind this is a maturing consensus:The more ubiquitous models become, the less failures should be discovered by luck. Today's AI incidents are scattered across customer support tickets, GitHub issues, and news comment sections, making it impossible to see the full picture. Without a uniform format there are no statistics, and without statistics there is no fixing priority.

When "reporting" becomes a standardized pipeline, AI reliability finally gains the potential to be systematically measured and tracked for the first time.

TechCrunch / Variety · Jul 4
Midjourney Strikes Back: Demanding Hollywood Hand Over Its Own AI Usage

Disney, Universal, and Warner Bros. copyright lawsuit against Midjourney escalates. Midjourney asks the court to compel studios to hand over AI business plans, training datasets, and board presentation materials. Image: TechCrunch / Getty.

Primary intelligence visual archive

Sued by Disney, Universal, and Warner Bros. Midjourney, Midjourney filed a motion this week to strike back. It asks the court to overturn the previous ruling that "studios only need to hand down consumer-facing AI usage logs" and instead demand the opposing side's AI business plans, research reports, training datasets, and board presentation materials.

Midjourney's lawyers argue:If the plaintiffs are secretly doing what they are suing others for, this is the core of the "unclean hands" defense.The studios responded that this is a "red herring," insisting their demand is to make Midjourney stop copying their characters. Plaintiffs seek statutory damages of up to 15 ten-thousand USD per infringed work, a scale capable of determining Midjourney's survival.

The focus of this lawsuit has escalated from "can AI learn from copyrighted works?" to "do the people suing AI use AI themselves?" The verdict could instantly expose the double standards across the creative industry.

Sources: TechCrunch / Variety · variety.com
