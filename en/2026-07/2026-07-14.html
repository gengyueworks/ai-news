07-14 · NVIDIA · Open-Source Flagship · Costs Plummet
NVIDIA Open-Sources Nemotron 3 Ultra, Slashing Inference Costs to One-Tenth of Closed-Source, But the Real Battle Has Just Begun

NVIDIA released the open-source large language model Nemotron 3 Ultra, with a total parameter count of 5500 billion, 55 billion active parameters per token, combining Mamba-2 and Mixture-of-Experts architecture, supporting 100 million token context.It achieved the highest accuracy in the open-source category on the LangChain deep agent benchmark, while its single-inference cost is only about one-tenth of leading closed-source models. NVIDIA does not intend to compete for API business with it; instead, it leverages deployable open-source weights to lock customers into the CUDA and hardware ecosystem. For enterprises and governments concerned about cross-border data transfer, this precisely addresses their pain points.

07-14 · OpenAI · Three Tiers Released · Cerebras Accelerated
OpenAI Deploys GPT-5.6 Across Three Tiers—Sol, Terra, and Luna—But the Real Battle Has Just Begun

OpenAI unveiled GPT-5.6 in three tiers: Sol, Terra, and Luna. The lightest tier, Sol, runs on Cerebras acceleration hardware, achieving a throughput of 750 token.Prior to release, it underwent an unusual customer-by-customer review by the Department of Commerce, with previews limited to about 20 approved institutions. With tiered pricing starting at $5 per million tokens for Sol, it brings the concept of 'same family, different scales' directly to the table. For buyers, this means selecting cheaper tiers based on the task, eliminating the need to continuously maintain the most expensive tier for occasional complex problems.

07-14 · Cognition · Coding Agent · RL Fine-Tuning
Cognition Launches SWE-1.7, Coding Agent Reaches 1,000 Tokens/s

Cognition released SWE-1.7, fine-tuned via reinforcement learning on Moonshot's open-source Kimi K2.7 base model, achieving a serving speed of 1000 tokens/s and boosting FrontierCode performance from 30.1% to 42.3%.Interestingly, it disclosed the base model origin—while the previous generation concealed its GLM base, this generation explicitly states it. Training open-source base models into functional products is becoming a standard path for coding agents: the model itself may not be the absolute strongest, but equipped with the right tools, it can execute engineering tasks on behalf of humans.

07-14 · IntCyberDigest / xAI · Trust incident
Grok Build accused of uploading entire private code repositories to the cloud—agent tools face scrutiny over what they are transmitting

xAI's Grok Build CLI has been criticized for uploading entire repositories—including private code and keys—to its Google Cloud bucket, far exceeding what is needed for coding tasks. Accusers also criticized its silent server-side mitigations and vague retention/deletion guarantees.

Primary intelligence visual archive

xAI's response emphasized ZDR (Zero Data Retention): when ZDR is enabled by the team, traces and code are not retained, and the /privacy command can disable retention and delete previously synced data.However, questions regarding whether default behaviors are reasonable, how past uploads are handled, and disclosure standards remain fully unresolved.

This discussion quickly escalated into a core argument in the open vs. closed-source debate: open-source advocates argue that the real issue is never cost, but control over the 'human-AI learning loop' and institutional knowledge. Arav Srinivas stated that the availability of ZDR was one of the reasons Perplexity quickly integrated Grok 4.5.

When agents start uploading your code to the cloud on your behalf, 'what it uploaded' will matter just as much as 'whether its answer was correct.'

Sources: Latent Space / AINews · IntCyberDigest
07-14 · PrismML · Compressed inference
27B model compressed to 3.9GB to fit on phones; local agentic workflows are no longer toys

PrismML releases Bonsai 27B, based on Qwen 3.6 27B: ternary version 5.9GB(approx. 1.71 effective bits), 1-bit version 3.9GB(approx. 1.125 effective bits), Apache 2.0. The selling point is that compression retains multimodal capabilities, tool calling, and long-context agentic workflows. Demos show Hermes running on an RTX 5090 and deployed on mobile devices.

Primary intelligence visual archive

On the same day, Tencent released 1-bit/4-bit Hy3, claiming that 295B flagship-scale models can be served on a single GPU using llama.cpp + MTP. Compression is pushing models close to the frontier onto consumer devices.

When 27B-class models fit in your pocket, local inference shifts from a "geek hobby" to a "viable foundation for serious agentic workflows."

The ceiling of model capabilities hasn't moved much, but the line of "which device tier can run these capabilities" is being pushed all the way down by quantization.

A 27B model compressed to 3.9GB fits in a phone: local agentic workflows are no longer toys.

Mistral open-sources Robostral Navigate, An 8B model teaches robots to walk using natural language

Mistral releases Robostral Navigate, the first open-weight model for embodied navigation, with 80 billion parameters. By compressing "understanding instructions and walking autonomously" into a small model, the barrier to robot deployment has been lowered once again.
