07-11 · OpenAI / Community Benchmarking · Post-Release Operations
GPT-5.6 is notoriously hard to choose, with 36 variants confusing users and OpenAI resetting quotas twice in two days.

OpenAI multiplied Sol/Terra/Luna tiers across multiple effort levels, leaving API users facing 36 configuration combinations. The community resorted to peer-to-peer tutorials: "Use luna high for daily coding, terra high for repo-wide changes, and don't touch Sol Extra." Sebastian Raschka's conclusion is straightforward:Unless you truly need Terra Ultra's performance, always use Luna with higher effort; results are identical yet cheaper..

Even more troublesome are the hidden costs: sub-agents inherit main session settings by default, and spawned Sol Ultras spawn more Sol Ultras, burning through quotas visibly. Multiple users reported: "5.6 is making me question whether the $200 Pro is worth it." OpenAI's response was unusually direct: resetting quotas twice, admitting default values pushed users toward expensive configs, and promising to revert to the familiar sidebar navigation.

Same-day data supported the idea that "using the right model is worth it": GPT-5.6 tied for first with Fable 5 on Code Arena Frontend, at roughly 2 times lower listed price; Presentation benchmark scored higher than GPT-5.5 by about 500 Elo.

The stronger the model, the more essential "choosing the right tier" becomes—what's wasted isn't scores, but budget.

07-11 · OpenAI · Health + Science
GPT-5.6's health narrative is concrete: Luna's lowest tier matches GPT-5.5's highest tier at 25x lower cost.

OpenAI has laid out two new narratives for GPT-5.6. Health: Luna matches GPT-5.5's peak effort performance at its lowest effort setting, while cutting costs by 25 x; in 2 tens of thousands of doctor blind reviews, physicians found fewer errors in GPT-5.6's outputs than in answers written by doctors themselves. Science: GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra reportedly used 64 sub-agents to generate a proof of the Cycle Double Cover conjecture within an hour (still awaiting independent verification).

Together, these two points point to a single move:packaging parallel research agents as "scientific computing primitives" and health as a vertical capability "cheap enough to scale.". The former is unverified, while the latter is backed by data.

The security side is also tightening concurrently: bio-vulnerability bounties are doubled to $50K and transitioned into a permanent program.

Simultaneously packaged as a research primitive, a medical assistant, and a security focal point, GPT-5.6's battlefield extends far beyond coding.
