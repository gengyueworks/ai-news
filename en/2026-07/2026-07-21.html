07-21 · Policy · Geopolitics

The US takes action against Chinese open-source models, more than just a ban, it's a comprehensive set of "tightening nets".

Axios reports that the US government is considering de facto restrictions on advanced Chinese open-source models (specifically naming Kimi), using tools including procurement limits, entity lists, security advisories, platform liability clauses, and public pressure.

The tech circle's reaction is one-sided:Hugging Face Clement Delangue, Palmer Luckey, Margaret Mitchell, and Bill Gurley all opposed, arguing that "restricting open source harms competition, harms sovereignty, and hurts defense even more."

The most concrete evidence comes from Hugging Face's self-disclosure — during a security incident, blocked by closed-source API guardrails from conducting forensic analysis, they had to switch to a locally deployed GLM-5.2 to get the job done.

This gives the notion that "open source is a security necessity" its first firsthand case:defenders need models that can be modified, run locally, and never refuse to answer..When authorities lock open source with "national security," defenders are locked out first — the direction of the ban is fighting its claimed objective.

Sources: AINews · Axios
07-21 · Math · Frontier Capabilities

AI finds counterexample to 3D Jacobian conjecture, another solid proof of "clearly superhuman" math tasks.

Multiple mathematicians and researchers reported that frontier models helped find a counterexample to the 3D Jacobian conjecture (a decades-long algebraic geometry problem).Alexei, a mathematician under Tian Gang who has long studied the problem, said frontier models are "already clearly superhuman on some mathematical tasks"; OpenAI internally also acknowledged that an internal Codex variant independently found almost the exact same counterexample and wrote a complete proof.

This is not the first time this trend has appeared this year —7 16 GPT-5.6 Sol just helped a UPenn professor use 90 minutes to overturn a pending 30 -year statistical conjecture. Two cases within a month,models are steadily moving from "reproducing the known" to "yielding new conclusions.".

The real shock lies in the pace:When what mathematicians need is no longer just "computing fast," but "providing suspicious directions worth exploring," researchers' work shifts from solving problems to judging which solution to trust.
