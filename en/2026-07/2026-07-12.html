07-10 · Frontier Lab · GPT-5.6 Tiering Confusion

GPT-5.6 Offers 36 Variants: Unquestionable Capabilities, Dazzling Bills

GPT-5.6 divides the model into Luna / Terra / Sol three tiers plus multiple effort levels, offering up to 36 combinations via API, while everyday users only see a single slider.The community consensus after empirical testing is "three tiers for three uses": Luna high for daily coding, Luna xhigh for better quality; Terra medium for medium tasks; and Terra high for cross-repository changes. Sebastian Raschka's rule of thumb is more direct:unless Terra Ultra-level performance is required, always use Luna with high effort settings for equivalent performance at a lower cost.

Primary intelligence visual archive

User complaints center on two main issues: first, 30 Too many combinations overwhelm users, and the lack of an "auto-routing" option; second is The $200 Pro plan is increasingly hard to justify, Sol, Terra, and Luna each have their own limits and pricing. You no longer just "pick the best model," you have to calculate how to spend wisely each month.

When models are sold by "tiers" rather than "models," pricing complexity itself becomes a new barrier for users.

07-11 · General · Distribution Rights and Risk Pricing

The "distribution rights" of AI models have become a new asset class, and risks are beginning to be explicitly priced.

The investment community is beginning to treat AI model "distribution rights" (who can pack models into what products and sell them to whom) and "risk pricing" (the cost of footing the bill for model misuse and failures) as independent investable themes.Models are no longer just technical assets; both their circulation channels and associated risks are being financialized. When insurance premiums, audit fees, and compliance costs start getting written into a model's ledger, the true cost of the AI business becomes visible for the first time.
