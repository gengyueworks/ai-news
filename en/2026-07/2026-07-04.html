Anthropic Turns Claude into a Workbench for Scientists

Claude Science, Anthropic's newly released AI workbench targets researchers. It is an integrated desktop app that connects researchers to standard tools and software packages, generates auditable artifacts, and provides flexible compute access. As TechCrunch aptly summarizes, Anthropic is betting on workflow, not just a new model.

Primary intelligence field evidence and architecture visual

Researchers can perform genomics analysis, CRISPR screening design, protein structure prediction, and cheminformatics without switching back and forth between PubMed, Jupyter, R, and cluster terminals. Every output includes a complete audit trail, making code, environments, and processes fully traceable.

This initiative extends beyond a single product. Previously Fable 5 and Mythos 5 regained availability, immediately followed by the launch of Claude Science. Anthropic's message is clear: restoring access to frontier models is just the first step; the ultimate prize is capturing entry points in high-value professional domains.

Claude Science integrates with over 60 pre-configured scientific skills and connectors, leveraging Evo 2, Boltz 2, and OpenFold3 from NVIDIA BioNeMo at the core.

Anthropic is also offering discounted Team plans for academic labs and funding 50 AI for Science project grants (up to 3 0,000 in credits + Modal 2000 USD computing power, as of July 15). Pro 20 USD/month, Max is 100 USD/month.

AI for science has historically remained at the demo or single-point capability stage. What stands out this time is end-to-end workflow integration, embedding AI directly into established working pipelines.

Alibaba Bans US AI Development Tools Entirely, Switches to In-House Qoder

Alibaba has announced a company-wide ban on external coding agent tools starting July 10, classifying them as high-risk software and mandating a transition to its proprietary product Qoder. The company previously discovered hidden code within the tool that checked time zones, proxy addresses, and API addresses without user awareness.Trust issues surrounding international AI tools are no longer a theoretical risk; they have become an internal security policy for China's largest tech companies.

Primary intelligence field evidence and architecture visual

Alibaba is not an isolated case. Following this incident, more Chinese enterprises are likely to reassess supply chain risks associated with relying on overseas AI development tools.Cross-border regulations are reshaping the landscape of developer tool selection.

LangChain · July 2

LangChain Open-Sources OpenWiki: Giving Agents a Self-Maintained Working Memory

'Wiki memory' is emerging as a practical design pattern for agents:LangChain introduces OpenWiki, a phrase openwiki --initto generate and continuously maintain agent-readable documentation for the codebase.

The motivation stems from a recurring pain point:Agents repeatedly lose working context across threads, requiring an inspectable knowledge layer rather than raw logs. Memory systems are also shifting from "retrieve-only" to "check-before-commit," where candidate memories are compared against existing ones and contradictions are resolved immediately rather than rehashed on every query.

When agents maintain their own wikis, memory ceases to be temporary conversation traces and becomes a growing asset.

Meta built a new app called Pocket: Generating shareable "gizmos" via AI prompts

Meta has launched a new social app called Pocket. Unlike legacy read-it-later tools, users create and share small interactive content via AI prompts, which Meta calls gizmos.

Google Play description: "Browse gizmo feeds from users worldwide. Gizmos respond to touch and phone tilt, play sound effects and music, access the camera or photo gallery, and some even perceive the surrounding world." Zuckerberg previously envisioned "creating and sharing interactive experiences with AI," and Pocket is the first implementation. Currently launched mainly in select regions outside the U.S.

AI social content is shifting from images/videos to playable interactive units. If gizmos truly flow within social feeds, they resemble next-gen lightweight app formats more than mere AI stickers.
