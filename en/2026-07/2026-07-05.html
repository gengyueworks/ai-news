A Lone Researcher Discovers a Linux Privilege Escalation Vulnerability Missed by Top AI

Bad Epoll (CVE-2026-46242): A use-after-free race condition hidden in the epoll system call. Credit: The Hacker News.

The newly disclosed Linux kernel vulnerability Bad Epoll(CVE-2026-46242) allows an unprivileged user to directly gain root access. It is hidden in the epoll subsystemrace condition code, classified as a use-after-free vulnerability. In the exact same code, Anthropic's flagship model Mythos previously found another bug but missed this one.

The vulnerability was discovered by independent security researcher Jaeyoung Chung and submitted as part of 0-day to Google 's kernelCTF program, which offers a minimum reward of 71,337 for kernel exploits.

Chung published GitHub a complete technical post-mortem, noting that the race window is "only about 6 instructions wide" and requires precise control of CPU scheduling and slab allocator states to reliably trigger.

The vulnerability affects Linux 6.4 and later versions, covering mainstream desktop distributions, servers, and newer Android devices. Chung's PoC achieves a reliability of 99%. The patch was completed by the kernel community within months, but the initial fix was incomplete, and the final correct patch took nearly two months to land. This incident once again exposes a reality: even the most advanced AI code review systems cannot replace human intuition for complex race conditions.

When the whole world claims that "AI will automatically discover vulnerabilities," this story provides the sharpest counterexample. In the same code, the AI looked and missed it, while a human patched it. Human judgment remains scarce.

GLM-5.2: 753B MoE, 1M token context, MIT license. Credit: Z.ai.

Electrek / The Information · July 2

Tesla caps AI spending for every engineer at 200 USD/week

Tesla is implementing a weekly AI spending cap of $200 for all employees starting July 6. Source: Electrek.

Even companies betting their future on AI are starting to hit the brakes on token bills. Starting in July, Tesla 6 starting from the date, implemented a weekly for all employees 200 USD AI spending cap, with amounts exceeding the limit requiring manager approval. Previously, some engineers were spending thousands of dollars per week on AI tools.

This news has resonated widely across the industry, as the advancement speed of AI capabilities far outpaces the catch-up rate of corporate governance systems. The most frequently cited negative example is Uber: The company once provided about 5000 engineers with unconstrained access to Claude Code, resulting in burning through 34 billion USD in AI budget within four months. Ultimately, Uber had to urgently halt and redesign its budget control workflow.

The real problem: AI return on investment has become a muddled mess. Every engineer can consume thousands of dollars in a matter of minutes with a lack of cost visibility—that is the root cause. This is not a technical problem; it is a management problem.

Reuters / Apptronik · June 30

Apptronik Opens 'Robot Park': Feeding Humanoid Robots with Real-World Data Factories

Apptronik's Apollo 2 humanoid robot. Pictured is the bipedal version, alongside a wheeled variant. Image: Apptronik.

Robotics company Apptronik has launched a nearly 9 ten-thousand-square-foot Robot Park, allowing fleets of Apollo 2 humanoid robots to repeatedly execute real manipulation tasks in realistic logistics, manufacturing, and retail environments while collecting training data. Apollo 2 offers both bipedal and wheeled forms to adapt to different scenario requirements.

Robot Park's data collection pipeline directly integrates with Google's DeepMind embodied AI model training pipeline. Apptronik also previewed the first true product-grade version Apollo 3, stating that it will achieve commercial mass production standards in reliability, cost, and deployment convenience.

The biggest bottleneck for humanoid robots has always been data, with a huge Sim-to-Real gap between lab simulation data and real-machine operation data. Robot Park's bet is that scaled collection of real-world operational data is the only path to bridge this gap.

The next battleground for large models is physical-world data. Whoever can collect real-world operational data at scale holds the fuel for embodied AI.

Variety / Deadline · Premiering June 10 · Hot This Week

First fully AI-generated feature film hits Tribeca Film Festival, also sparking boycotts

Still from "Dreams of Violets": The film uses AI video tools to reconstruct the 2026 Iran protests based on news reports and eyewitness testimony. Photo: Variety.

A feature-length docudrama with every single frame generated by AI, running 75 minutes, titled "Dreams of Violets", becoming New York's Tribeca Film Festivalfirst all-AI feature film in history.

Directed by tech entrepreneur brothers Ash and Pooya Koosha, the film draws on the January 2026 nationwide protests in Iran and the security forces' crackdown, with international organizations estimating the death toll exceeding 7000. All visuals were generated by AI video tools based on news reports, photographs, and eyewitness testimonies.

The film sparked intense debate at the festival. On one side, tech pioneers cheered it as a milestone for AI storytelling; on the other, numerous filmmakers and Iranian-American artists staged public boycotts, accusing the film of "stealing real victims' stories and replacing human creators with AI."

The Koosha brothers defended their work, stating they aimed to use AI to bring attention to tragedies overlooked by mainstream media, while critics argued this precisely deprives victims and journalists of the right to tell their own stories.

As production barriers collapse, what becomes scarce is who has the right to speak, what is told, and who bears the cost.

TechCrunch / Menlo Ventures · July 4

Patronus AI's 5000 M Series B: Stress-Testing AI Agents with Digital Twin Worlds

Patronus AI Completed 5000 M Series B, valuation undisclosed. Its approach is unique: digital world models, using high-fidelity digital twins of real-world systems to stress-test AI agents.

Traditional AI safety testing relies on static benchmarks; Patronus has AI agents run autonomously in a simulated, complete business environment to observe whether they exhibit dangerous behaviors in complex scenarios.

The value of this technology is especially pronounced as agents see large-scale deployment—you cannot afford to let a fallible agent trial-and-error on live customer data. Patronus's investors include Lightspeed and NEA. The company states its clients span finance, healthcare, and e-commerce, with some having integrated digital world model testing into their CI/CD pipelines.

As agents move from demo to production, the value of the verification layer grows exponentially; whoever controls safety verification holds pricing power.
