07-08 · Axios / Reuters · Exclusive
GPT-5.6 Approved for Wide Release, Global Launch on Thursday, But the Real Battle Has Just Begun

The U.S. Department of Commerce has approved OpenAI for the widespread release of the GPT-5.6 series.Confirmed by multiple sources following an exclusive Axios report: Three models Sol, Terra, Luna will open to all users on Thursday (July 9). Following emergency access restrictions requested by the government last month and weeks of additional safety testing under the CAISI framework, the federal government has finally cleared the release.

This approval marks a turning point—previously, GPT-5.6 was restricted to a select group of vetted partners with details reported to authorities. Opening it globally signals a shift in the U.S. government's AI safety review framework from "blocking" to "test-and-release."3 models underpin this release.

  • Three models: Sol (Flagship), Terra, Luna
  • Previously restricted due to national security concerns, released after additional government testing
  • CAISI Framework: U.S. Frontier AI Regulation Shifts from "Blocking" to "Test-and-Release"
  • Preview access opens globally today; official release on July 9

07-01 (Ongoing) · Google Blog · Major NotebookLM Update
NotebookLM Transforms into an AI Workbench: Cloud Code + Auto Charts + Source Search, But the Real Battle Has Just Begun

NotebookLM has evolved from a note-taking assistant into an AI research environment equipped with a workbench.In a late June update, Google equipped NotebookLM with Gemini 3.5, a secure cloud computer (capable of writing code to run analysis), and integrated Google Search for automated source discovery. Users can start with a vague idea and let NotebookLM gather materials, process data, and generate PDF reports or budget spreadsheets.

Primary intelligence visual archive

Two practical changes stand out: first, hundreds of preset software skills enable the model to handle professional tasks (data cleaning, format conversion, chart generation); second, output formats expand to PDF reports, spreadsheets, and coursework papers, with support for post-generation editing. For those who frequently need to build research frameworks from scratch, this is more than an upgrade—it is becoming a new work portal.

  • Powered by Gemini 3.5 + Antigravity, with upgraded reasoning and accuracy
  • Built-in cloud computer for writing code and running analysis (100+ software skills)
  • Web-searchable for automated source finding, building research from vague ideas
  • Output formats: PDF reports, charts, spreadsheets, course materials, with post-edit support
  • Available to AI Ultra and Workspace enterprise customers
Sources: Google Blog

07-07 · The Verge · Emma Roth
Google Earth Pro Departs: Closing the Chapter on Desktop GIS

Google has announced it will discontinue downloads for the Google Earth Pro desktop application after June 25, 2027.Born in the early 2000s, this desktop GIS tool was once a window to the world for countless geography, architecture, and environmental professionals—from 3D building models and historical imagery to GIS data import and high-res printing. After going free in 2015, it became one of the most widely used professional mapping tools.

Primary intelligence visual archive

The immediate reason is Google's resource shift toward the web version, but the deeper signal is that the desktop GIS era is being replaced by cloud- and AI-driven spatial computing. Just as Google Earth Pro opened an era, its departure marks the beginning of a new phase.

  • Downloads stop June 25, 2027; installed users can continue using it
  • Google recommends migrating to Google Earth for Web
  • Desktop GIS tools replaced by cloud + AI spatial computing
Sources: The Verge

Diverging AI Capital Markets: Approvals, Flat Openings, Valuation Scrutiny, and Power Pivots

Today's news paints a unified picture: capital is flowing into AI, but investors are picking and choosing.

Primary intelligence visual archive

OpenAI secured Ministry of Commerce approval with GPT-5.6 rolling out globally—a regulatory green light. On the same day, Momenta opened nearly flat on the HKEX, shifting AI IPOs from "blind chasing" to "wait-and-see." A day earlier, Morgan Stanley argued that semiconductor rallies are misdirected, advising a shift from chips to cloud providers actually profiting from AI.

This week's top funding went to Houston energy firm Joulent (17.5 billion strategic funding), while humanoid robotics startup Zeroth secured an 5 100M+ RMB round led by Ant Group—money is shifting from "renting models" to "building bodies, powering facilities, and deploying." Three signals point to the same conclusion: AI investment has entered a "picky" phase, focusing on who turns capital into revenue first.
