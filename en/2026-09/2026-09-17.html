Returning from Several Weeks Away, Nathan Lambert Finds Regular People Still Experience AI as a Rounding Error

Nathan Lambert treated his wedding break as a natural control experiment, disconnecting entirely from AI discourse. The feedback he gathered was stark: the public's touchpoints with AI remain peripheral or largely negligible; many had vaguely heard about OpenAI and Hugging Face headlines, but could rarely articulate what they meant.

Favorable impressions mostly centered on playful generated images and upgraded web searches, while negative sentiment stemmed from addictive recommendation feeds, friends glued to chatbots, and community complaints over power-hungry data centers. His takeaway: the Industrial Revolution analogy holds up regarding technical scale, but falls apart regarding everyday exposure, especially as society today displays much stronger structural inertia against rapid change.

Why Home Robots Still Haven't Arrived Traces Back to 2023 and the Original RT-2 Celebrity Demo

Timothy B. Lee revisits Google's RT-2 milestone. When researchers wired a vision-language model into a robotic manipulator arm, the system recognized celebrity photos completely absent from the training set and converted them into physical actions. Team member Karol Hausman recalled that this was the exact moment they realized the paradigm could work.

RT-2 established the vision-language-action (VLA) paradigm as an industry benchmark, spurring American and Chinese tech giants alongside startups to pour billions of dollars into the space over the following three years. Yet the foundational gaps left unresolved in 2023 remain bottlenecks today: the real-world deployment of embodied AI remains stalled on the subtle challenges of out-of-distribution generalization and error tolerance.
