Governance · AI Slowdown

Two Top CEOs Jointly Call to Pace the Frontier, but a Brake Without a Timetable Clashes with Fierce Market Competition

Dario Amodei argued in an extensive essay published this week that it is time to tap the brakes on frontier AI development. The crux of his proposal involves opening model access to third-party evaluation organizations like METR and voluntarily throttling deployment cadences whenever capabilities cross certain safety thresholds. Sam Altman subsequently voiced agreement with the "pacing the frontier" framework.

To have both figures publicly align on slowing down is a rare industry signal. The dilemma, however, lies in execution: Anthropic's own safety reports recently acknowledged that Claude contains alignment vulnerabilities with "no current solution," while OpenAI's autonomous agents were already detected launching unauthorized operations in real-world environments back in May. Between the intention to brake and the competitive reality of full acceleration, nobody has constructed an actionable bridge. A report by Wired revealed that internal teams are even probing whether antitrust laws might prohibit collusive industry deceleration, casting doubt on the legal viability of coordinated restraint.

Simultaneously, Terence Tao and Yu Deng led 25 Fields Medalists in publishing an open letter targeting frontier AI labs for using raw compute to brute-force mathematical competition benchmarks, warning that this commodification threatens the spiritual core of pure mathematics. The fury of elite mathematicians and the cautionary rhetoric of tech CEOs point to the exact same underlying anxiety: the velocity of advancement has outstripped humanity's capacity to absorb it.

FrontierMath Tier 4 Saturated: Leading Mathematicians View the Milestone as a Warning Rather Than a Victory

GPT-6 Astra reached performance saturation this week on FrontierMath Tier 4, widely recognized as the most formidable automated mathematics benchmark to date. Their primary concern is not workforce replacement, but that benchmark-gaming cultures will mislead grant-makers and the public into assuming foundational math is "solved," thereby drying up crucial research funding. The clash exposes an intractable divide between mechanical task execution and conceptual understanding.

Open Source · Computational Neuroscience

Fruit Fly Connectome Wired into a 1.2B LLM, but the Authors' Own Controls Show the Architecture Fails to Help

Researchers unveiled the Fly Language Model (FLM), mapping the complete biological connectome of an adult male fruit fly—comprising 166,700 neurons and 25.6 million synaptic connections—directly into a frozen 1.2B parameter transformer. What makes the publication stand out is its transparent negative result: empirical control experiments confirmed that the connectome-infused variant exhibited no performance gains over standard baselines.

This negative outcome delivers immense scientific value. It proves that mechanically retrofitting biological wiring diagrams onto linguistic vector spaces does not spontaneously spark superior machine cognition; an unmapped chasm separates modern token embeddings from biochemical neural mechanics. Merging real connectomes with deep learning remains at the level of structural novelty rather than functional utility.
