Employees spend more time "babysitting AI" than actually working with it; half grow stronger with AI, while a quarter grow weaker; enterprises are simultaneously spooked by AI bills and lured by cheap Chinese models.
Read This First · Plain English Edition

In plain terms:
First, using AI doesn't necessarily save time, with employees spending 6.4 hours a week "babysitting" AI (feeding data, fixing errors, checking outputs)—more than the time spent actually working with it.
Second, AI doesn't level human gaps; it widens them. With AI, half of people improve, a quarter stay the same, and a quarter actually get worse.
Third, AI bills are changing corporate choices, as U.S. companies, spooked by AI costs, start turning to Chinese models that are several times cheaper.

Jargon Decoder:

Botsitting≈ Employees spending time "waiting on" AI: feeding data, fixing errors, and checking its work. This is uncounted, invisible labor.

AI Agent≈ AI that doesn't just chat, but actually does work for you: writing code, booking itineraries, and processing spreadsheets.

token≈ The smallest unit of text processed by AI, also the metric for usage-based billing. The more you use, the higher the bill.

Forward Deployment Engineer (FDE)≈ Specialists who help enterprises implement AI effectively; both OpenAI and Anthropic are hiring for this.

DEEP DIVE
What people are actually experiencing in the AI workplace
1. AI doesn't save time; it shifts it

Botsitting is the most overlooked workplace phenomenon of the past six months:Employees spend 6.4 hours a week "tending to" AI—more time than they spend actually working with it. Feeding data, fixing errors, and checking outputs are all uncounted, unpaid invisible labor.

Companies thought buying AI would buy back employee time, but instead, employees use the saved time to babysit AI.。

2. AI amplifies gaps instead of leveling the playing field

A KPMG study of 523 people found that:Foundational skills like critical thinking, AI literacy, and domain knowledge don't determine who uses AI well. What truly creates the gap is "how to collaborate with AI": those who proactively orchestrate workflows, question outputs, and treat AI as a thought partner (amplifiers) far exceed the baseline; those who accept AI output without scrutiny (delegators) perform on par; and those who hinder rather than help (apprentices) perform even worse.

AI hasn't standardized work; instead, it has widened the gap between those who know how to collaborate and those who don't.。

III. Bills are changing corporate choices

Uber burned through its budget, Walmart is capping tokens, and one company spends 500 million tokens a month.US corporate AI bills are so large they are forced to pinch pennies. Enter China's GLM-5.2: good enough, and several times cheaper.

A RAND researcher once said:"The feared scenario is a model from China that is good enough and costs a quarter of the price". When companies are forced to make choices by their bills, price becomes the biggest variable.
