Mission Accomplished, What's Next:
Why Google Dissolved the AlphaFold Team
2026-08-07 · Special Report 8 Reports Linked · 1 In-Depth Analysis
Established eight years ago to tackle a scientific grand challenge, it disbanded eight years later with 200 million predictions and a Nobel Prize.

Google DeepMind has dismantled its own AlphaFold team.

The scientific community says this comes as no surprise.
Read this first · Plain English version

Three takeaways in plain English:
First, an AI named AlphaFold won the Nobel Prize—it solved the problem of "guessing what proteins look like," a decades-long bottleneck in biology.
Second, the AI team was dissolved by Google—not because it failed, but precisely because it succeeded and the mission was accomplished.
Third, this reflects a major pivot in Google's AI strategy—shifting from the "London lab" back to "California business," complete with a new CEO.

Glossary of terms:

AlphaFold ≈ An AI built by Google that uses computers to predict the 3D shapes of proteins, winning the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

Protein folding problem ≈ A decades-old biological riddle: given a chain of amino acids, figuring out its folded 3D structure.

DeepMind ≈ Google's AI lab, creator of AlphaFold, headquartered in London.

Isomorphic Labs ≈ Google's spin-off AI drug discovery company, built on AlphaFold research.

DEEP DIVE
A Disbandment Without Sorrow
I. The disbandment happened, but the legacy remains

The day after the FT report, a DeepMind spokesperson gave a calm response to Scientific American: many personnel changes occurred a year ago or even earlier, and AlphaFold's public database and prediction servers remain open.

The team has dispersed, but the achievements live on. The protein structure library predicted by AlphaFold has already become foundational infrastructure for structural biology researchers worldwide. Some are using it to develop malaria vaccines, others to improve crops,Isomorphic Labs a drug discovery company built upon AlphaFold's research.

II. Why the disbandment: Because the mission is accomplished

To understand this, we first need to look at how DeepMind defines AlphaFold. John Moult, co-founder of the CASP competition and computational biologist at the University of Maryland, John Moult, put it bluntly: from the very beginning, DeepMind picked a problem that could "prove success or failure in a clean way," setting out to prove it not only to the world, but to themselves.

As a result,2020 AlphaFold2 won that proof. Moult remarked back then that the structure prediction problem was essentially solved. DeepMind knew it too:"They did it. What's the next task?"

For a research institution organized around "grand challenges," AlphaFold was a problem, a team, and a goal from its inception. Once the problem was solved, the team dissolved, and people flowed to the next problem. There is no sentimentality in this, only process.

III. Free Access and Google's Ledger

Another backdrop to the disbandment is money. Demis Hassabis insisted on making AlphaFold freely available; while this decision won Google immense prestige, it also accumulated friction within the company:huge breakthroughs, very little revenue.。

Meanwhile, DeepMind's strategic focus is shifting. Vice President of Research Pushmeet Kohliexplained to the FT: over the past nine years, the lab organized teams around "grand challenges," but the strategy has now changed to building Gemini-powered scientific assistant systems and competing with OpenAI and Anthropic for the frontier agent space.

AlphaFold is the perfect product of the old strategy, and also the first thing to be put down in the new one.

IV. Why the Scientific Community Isn't Surprised

Harvard Medical School computational biologist Debora Marks' response was almost cold:"I would do the exact same thing. It's already done, why keep going?"

More importantly, the baton for AlphaFold had already been passed on long ago.2024 That year, DeepMind open-sourced the inference code for AlphaFold3 and made its weights available to academia. Independent research groups have already built their own versions and extensions. Moult said he doesn't think the disbandment will slow down the work,"because other people are doing it now too."。

V. Much Remains Unsolved

Yet "essentially solved" applies only to a specific benchmark; vast areas of structural biology remain unfinished. Proteins are often part of larger molecular machines, shifting between multiple shapes while working, making it still very difficult for researchers to predict these dynamic states and how molecules bind to them.

Moult said:"There's a long list of such questions that are definitely not fully resolved."

These problems are still waiting for a new AlphaFold. Only next time, it might not be Google doing it.

6. The disbanding of AlphaFold is just a corner of Google's larger pivot

Zooming out, the dissolution of the AlphaFold team is part of DeepMind's power handover. After Demis Hassabis was promoted to Alphabet Chief Scientist, DeepMind's new helm Kavukcuoglu took over an operation much larger than a protein prediction team: Google poured about 1900 billion dollars into AI last year, yet has failed to turn a Transformer-level scientific breakthrough into a product first-mover advantage like ChatGPT.

The FT says Google's AI footprint is the broadest in Silicon Valley—so broad it needs refocusing; Bloomberg says Hassabis's departure takes away the only person outside California controlling an American AI giant's R&D. Two lines pointing to the same thing:Google's AI focus is shifting from "labs in London" back to "business in California"。

AlphaFold is the first name dropped in this shift of focus. It was dropped because it has completed its mission. What is truly worth watching is how Google handles those unfinished sciences next, and those things that, like AlphaFold, once carried high hopes of "proving Google."
