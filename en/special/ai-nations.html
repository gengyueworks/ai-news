AI is a global race, but every country plays it differently.

Leveraging post-Brexit perks to become the "European home for AI unicorns," the UK still lags far behind a fraction of the US; China is challenging the US computing power narrative with open source and cost-efficiency; the US is engaged in an arms race alongside export controls; Africa and Latin America have yet to secure a ticket to the game.

This series explores the positions and dilemmas of various countries in the AI race.
Read This First · Plain English Edition

Three takeaways in plain English:
First, there are only three AI powerhouses: the US, China, and half of Europe; other countries are either catching up or watching from the sidelines.
Second, every country has a different "playbook": the US burns cash to scale computing power, China leverages open source for cost-efficiency, and the UK aims to be the application layer intermediary.
Third, the AI gap is global; those who squeeze into the supply chain feast, while those left out lack even enough electricity.

Jargon Glossary:

Unicorns≈ Startups valued over $1 billion, serving as a metric for a country's AI vitality.

Computing Power≈ The "engine" for training AI; whoever has more compute runs faster, but it consumes massive amounts of money and electricity.

Open-Source Models≈ Open-sourcing AI "blueprints" for free, so anyone can download and use them. China's play.

AI Supply Chain≈ The chain needed to build AI: chips, compute, data centers. Countries that squeeze in get the spoils.

DEEP DIVE
The Global Landscape of AI
I. United States: The Frontrunner and the Gatekeeper

The US has the most cash-burning arms race ($ 7000 billion in annual capital expenditure by the big four cloud providers) and competition like Anthropic overtaking OpenAI. But more importantly:The US government bars non-Americans from accessing Anthropic's latest models; access to frontier AI is determined by the US government.。

The US is not just an AI powerhouse, but the "gatekeeper" of AI. Who gets to use the latest AI is decided by the US.。

II. China: Open Source and Cost-Performance to Bypass Compute Blockades

Unable to buy Nvidia's best chips, China builds its own (Huawei Ascend is faster than H20); meanwhile, it disrupts the market with open-source and low prices. 50-150%DeepSeek V4 Pro offers three-quarters of the performance at one-sixtieth of the cost, prompting British economists to note:Most applications do not require the latest models.。

China's strategy is "good enough + cheap + open source," using cost-performance to bypass US compute blockades.。

III. United Kingdom: Ambition and Awkwardness in the Middle Tier

The UK has 33 AI unicorns, Brexit dividends, and what Jensen Huang calls the "best position for the application layer." But compared to the US it's a drop in the bucket, power prices are 4 times those in Texas, and it cannot even access the latest models.The UK wants a slice of the "application layer," but is squeezed between US compute power and Chinese cost-performance.。

IV. The Left-Behinds: Africa and Latin America

Rogoff's most sobering observation:Africa lacks power, Latin America lacks capital, and India's outsourcing industry bears the brunt.90% of AI computing power is concentrated in the US and China.The global AI map is splitting into "countries that eat meat" and countries "that don't even have enough electricity."。

V. Asia's Middle Path: Singapore's Answer

Singapore has chosen a third path: neither taking sides in the compute race nor acting as a mere bystander. Singapore's National Day message made it clear—new AI opportunities must be seized, productivity boosted, and job quality raised, but "not every new technology can be accepted blindly."Growth continues (Q2 GDP up 5.7% year-on-year) while maintaining strategic options.。

While the US and China compete on raw compute, Singapore focuses on "selective adoption"—arguably the most pragmatic answer for a resource-constrained nation.。
