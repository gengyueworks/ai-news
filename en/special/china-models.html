The Push of Chinese Open-Source Models:
DeepSeek, Kimi, Zhipu, MiniMax, and Qwen Play Their Hands Simultaneously
2026-06 → 08 · Special Report 5 Companies · 10 Milestones · 1 Main Thread
From June to August, five Chinese AI companies rolled out their flagship models back-to-back.

Zhipu first put the "cheap Chinese agent" on the table with GLM-5.2, DeepSeek made V4-Flash "good enough and cheap," Kimi K3 maxed out servers with 2.8 trillion parameters, MiniMax H3 enabled local video generation for the first time, and Alibaba turned the 2.4T Qwen3.8-Max into a usable product first.

Before this, Chinese models were called "chasers"; after this, the price wars, open-source battles, and compute narratives were all completely reshuffled.
Read This First · Plain English Edition

Where is AI at right now?What happened this week can be boiled down to three sentences:
First, the "intelligence level" of AI can now write code, generate video, and read 700,000-word long texts, and it's not a demo—it's free and open for anyone to download and run.
Second, prices have been slashed to "practically free"; the cost of processing 1 million tokens (about 750,000 Chinese characters) is so low it's a fraction of a cent, making top-tier AI affordable for everyday people.
Third, for the first time, Chinese companies are collectively leading on the "open-source" track; five Chinese companies released their flagship models in succession, even causing U.S. chip stocks to drop in a panic.

Jargon Cheat Sheet:

Parameters (304B / 2.8T)≈ The model's "brain capacity." A larger number usually means smarter, but more expensive and slower.

Open Source / Open Weights≈ Publishing the model's "brain blueprint" for free, allowing anyone to download, modify, and run it themselves.

Multimodal≈ A single model can simultaneously understand and generate text, images, audio, and video.

token≈ The minimum unit for a model to process text; 1 million tokens equals approximately 750,000 Chinese characters.

DEEP DIVE
This week, Chinese AI models changed their playbook
1. Similarities and differences among five companies

In the same week, five companies took five different actions: DeepSeek bet on cost-effectiveness (using 13B active parameters for Agent tasks), Kimi bet on scale (fully open-sourcing 2.8T), MiniMax bet on multimodal (direct 2K video generation), Alibaba bet on commercialization (prioritizing API products), and Zhipu had earlier put "affordable Chinese agents" on the table with GLM-5.2.

The commonalities are more telling:All five are bundling "open-source + low-price + usability" instead of just chasing benchmarks. This is no coincidence. The 2025 price war taught everyone that high model scores alone aren't enough—developers need to actually use them.

2. Why a "week" instead of a "year"

The concentrated moves by the five companies within 7 days stem from a shared milestone:Late July is the convergence point for their training compute, funding arrivals, and product schedules.DeepSeek's first major technological delivery after securing funding, Moonshot AI's open-sourcing following its Series F round, and Alibaba's flagship transitioning to commercial use all signal that "the money has arrived, time to deliver."

The Financial Times' assessment of Alibaba hits the mark:"This race is not just about building top-tier AI, but about building AI that can turn a profit."。

III. Kimi K3 Shakes U.S. Stocks: Chinese Models Become a Macro Variable for the First Time

On August 2, the day Kimi K3 was open-sourced, Nvidia's core suppliers and AI data center REITs were sold off, with JPMorgan Chase calling it the "DeepSeek 2.0 moment." A U.S. stock fund manager remarked:"For the first time, we are pricing a Chinese model as a systematic variable."。

The weight of this statement surpasses any benchmark:When open-source weights become so cheap that "the barrier for enterprises to build their own server rooms drops," long-term cloud computing lease agreements begin to be recalculated. For the first time, the cost-effectiveness of Chinese models has shaken competitors' computing power narrative.。

IV. Zhipu's Strategic Move: Breaching the Market Through "Affordability"

Zhipu's GLM-5.2 beat others to the punch, embedding the term "cheap Chinese AI" into Silicon Valley's consciousness. The Atlantic put it bluntly: Just as major U.S. tech firms finally convinced enterprises to pay for AI agents, the bills were so exorbitant—running into thousands of dollars per employee per month, with Uber burning through its entire annual budget in just a few months—that when an open-source model several times cheaper with competitive coding capabilities was handed over, it proved more effective than any marketing.

Once Chinese enterprises turn "cost-effective agent capabilities" into reality, the remaining battleground is who can convert this wave of affordability into genuine market share.。

V. The Aftermath: Open-Sourcing Is Just the Real Beginning

Open-sourcing is merely the first step. After releasing the weights, local deployment, fine-tuning, toolchain adaptation, ComfyUI integration, and developer reputation are the battlefields that determine "whether open-source can truly thrive." MiniMax H3 took only 3 days from release to open-sourcing, but a long road remains to get creators to actually use it for filmmaking.

DeepSeek's V4-Flash was deployed into its own agent pipeline, allowing agents to autonomously run scripts that previously took a team a full day to write.The next stop for Chinese models is shifting from "easy-to-use models" to "models that can do the work for humans."。
