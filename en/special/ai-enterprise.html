Enterprise AI:
Efficiency vs. Growth Are Two Different Things
Continuously Updated · Enterprise AI Series Introduction · 6 Clues
Ask a round of executives what AI can do for enterprises, and the answers almost all point to the same word: efficiency.

Cutting costs, reducing headcount, and speeding up processes. But this may be a misconception. HBR research shows that what truly doubles enterprise value is growth, not efficiency.

This series discusses how enterprises should actually use AI: where money is spent, how returns are calculated, and how organizations must change.
Read This First · Plain English Edition

Three straightforward takeaways:
First, using AI for efficiency has a ceiling on returns, as costs can only be cut to zero, but revenue can grow infinitely. Cost-cutting boosts valuation by at most 10%, while driving growth can add 50% to 135%.
Second, enterprise AI bills are spiraling out of control, with Uber burning through its entire annual AI budget in months, and one company accidentally spending $500 million on token fees in a single month.
Third, the most expensive part of AI isn't compute, but the hidden time spent by humans "babysitting" AI, with employees spending 6.4 hours a week feeding data, fixing errors, and checking outputs.

Jargon Glossary:

Efficiency≈ Saving costs, headcount, and time with AI, but with capped returns.

Growth≈ Making more money with AI (acquiring customers, improving quality, expanding market share), with limitless returns.

Growth Blindspot≈ Enterprises know AI drives growth, but they invest all their money in efficiency, neglecting growth.

valuation multiple≈ The premium the market is willing to pay for a company's future growth; the faster the growth, the higher the multiple.

If you are a business decision-maker trying to figure out "where exactly AI should be invested," this is the starting point for the answer.

DEEP DIVE
Where Enterprises Should Spend Their AI Budgets
I. The Growth Blind Spot: Efficiency Gains Have a Ceiling

HBR ran the numbers:Cost-cutting boosts an enterprise's valuation by at most 10%, whereas driving growth can increase it by 50% to 135%. The reason is simple math: costs can only be cut to zero, while revenue has limitless upside. Moreover, the market prices enterprises based on future growth expectations, not how much money was saved today.

However, they discovered a "growth blind spot":Executives believe AI can double enterprise value, yet almost no one allocates AI investments to growth. It's all spent on efficiency.

II. The Bill: AI Costs Are Finally Too Big to Ignore

Uber burned through its entire annual budget, Walmart capped tokens, and one company spent 500 million tokens a month.As AI shifts from "per-seat subscriptions" to "usage-based pricing," costs have become significant enough for enterprises to take serious stock for the first time. Meanwhile, China's GLM-5.2 has arrived—good enough and several times cheaper.

A RAND researcher's assessment is that the future norm may be "China offering models that are good enough at one-quarter of the price."。

III. The Most Expensive Cost Is "Humans Babysitting AI"

When calculating AI budgets, enterprises missed the largest item:Employees spending 6.4 hours a week "tending to" AI. Feeding data, fixing errors, and checking outputs—this invisible labor doesn't appear on any balance sheet, yet it is more expensive than the compute bill.

A KPMG study twists the knife further:Using the same AI, half the people get stronger while a quarter actually get weaker. Enterprises buy AI, but what they get is a divergence in human-machine collaboration capabilities.。

IV. The Reverse: AI Makes Solopreneurship Possible

The exact same AI translates to "cost-cutting" for an enterprise, but "starting a company" for an individual:Founders using AI to help build startups doubled in two years to 60%. Stripe economists say AI fills skill gaps that once required hiring, ushering in the "era of the solopreneur."

While enterprises still debate where to invest in AI, individuals are already using it to compress a whole company into a single person. For enterprises, AI is a cost center; for individuals, it's a startup leverage.。
