Microsoft Build Day 2: Introducing MAI-Thinking-1, Its First Reasoning Model—Finally Its Own Engine

On the second day of Microsoft Build, Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman officially released MAI-Thinking-1, Microsoft's first in-house reasoning model. 35B active parameters, 1T total parameters, 256K context window. Microsoft's internal blind tests show user preference is on par with Claude Sonnet 4.6.

Key Context: Over the past two years, virtually all of Microsoft's AI products—Copilot, GitHub, Azure—have run on OpenAI's models.MAI-Thinking-1 marks the first time Microsoft has its own reasoning model to compete directly against frontier rivals in the same arena. Also released are MAI-Code-1-Flash (a coding model specifically for GitHub Copilot with 5B active parameters) and seven other models covering vision, speech, and transcription.

Suleyman also shared cost data: after tuning for McKinsey, the MAI models surpass GPT-5.5 in quality, with cost efficiency reportedly reaching 10x that of OpenAI. Microsoft's official statement is that "running proprietary models on Azure eliminates third-party inference fees."

What It Means for You: Microsoft has transitioned from a model buyer to a model builder. This isn't abandoning OpenAI—it's doing both. However, for developers, a third optional reasoning engine (Microsoft + OpenAI + Open Source) will soon be available on Azure.

Sources: Euronews · The Verge · The Letter Two · Build Fast With AI

Microsoft Majorana 2 Quantum Chip: 1,000x Reliability Boost—Three Years Away from a Usable Quantum Computer

Nadella announced in his Build keynote Majorana 2, Microsoft's second-generation quantum processor. If Majorana 1 proved that "basic physics is feasible," Majorana 2 marks "the start of engineering." The timeline given by Nadella:Scalable quantum computer by 2029.

1,000x reliability improvement—a metric from Microsoft's internal testing. Also released is the Aion 1.0 series: two small models (14B parameters) specifically designed to run AI agents locally on Windows devices, currently in preview.

Sources: Build Fast With AI · A Guide to Cloud
OpenAI Models and Codex Fully Available on AWS—Azure No Longer the Sole Gateway

OpenAI's GPT-5.5, GPT-5.4, and Codex have transitioned from limited preview to Generally Available (GA) on Amazon Bedrock. This means any AWS customer can directly invoke OpenAI frontier models via the Bedrock console, without needing a separate OpenAI account or Azure subscription.

Pricing matches OpenAI's official rates with no extra fees, and usage counts toward existing AWS cloud commitments. Weekly active users for Codex have surpassed 5 million—up 1 million from 4 million during the April preview. OpenAI also teased that the Daybreak cybersecurity initiative will be the next capability to land on AWS.

Background: On April 27, Microsoft and OpenAI amended their contract to end the Azure exclusivity clause; the very next day, OpenAI and AWS announced their partnership. GA launched on June 1, with widespread media coverage on June 3. Exactly five weeks from contract amendment to general availability.

Sources: OpenAI · Memeburn · A Guide to Cloud

Sam Altman Interviewed Live at Stargate Datacenter: Coding Models Currently the Largest Demand Driver

CNBC interviewed Sam Altman live at the Stargate datacenter in Saline, Michigan. Stargate is one of OpenAI's major AI infrastructure projects currently under construction, with this site alone costing $16.0 billion in development and construction, plus another $30 billion earmarked for internal hardware.

One of Altman's most candid remarks:"Coding models are the single largest demand driver. They became remarkably good between late last year and early this year, and took another major leap in recent months." He also admitted underestimating model "jaggedness"—performing exceptionally well at some tasks while still exhibiting weak supervision capabilities for long-term, complex workflows. He noted a notable phenomenon: the companies using the most AI are also the ones hiring the most people.

The Oracle CEO echoed this sentiment on-site: "Demand right now exceeds what we are able to deliver."

Sources: CNBC · Smart Content Report
GPT-5.5 Generally Available on Microsoft Foundry

On the same day, OpenAI's GPT-5.5 became Generally Available (GA) on Microsoft Foundry, including the GPT-5.5 Pro version. Pricing: GPT-5.5 is $5/million input · $30/million output; Pro version is $30/million input · $180/million output. Eligible for Azure Consumption Commitment deduction.

The Subtlety: On the very same day, OpenAI's models became GA simultaneously on AWS and Azure. The era of exclusivity has ended, and OpenAI has officially become a multi-cloud provider.

Microsoft Releases Aion 1.0—Small Models Running Agents Locally on Windows Laptops

Microsoft released the Aion 1.0 series, consisting of two small models (14B parameters): Aion 1.0 Instruct and Aion 1.0 Plan. Positioned to provide local AI agent capabilities for Windows devices—independent of the cloud, running directly on laptops.

Also released Microsoft Scout is the first "Autopilot"—an always-on personal agent running across Windows and Mac, reading Teams, Outlook, calendars, OneDrive, and SharePoint to coordinate schedules, identify workflow bottlenecks, and handle them proactively.You can think of it as an AI assistant built on the OpenClaw tech stack running permanently in the background.

Sources: The Verge · A Guide to Cloud
