NVIDIA Open-Sources Nemotron 3 Ultra: 550B Parameters, Built Specifically for Long-Running Agents

At Computex 2026, NVIDIA announced Nemotron 3 Ultra, a MoE model featuring 550 billion total parameters and 55 billion active parameters. This is no ordinary LLM—it is the first open-source frontier model designed specifically for "long-running agents.".

The architecture is notable:Hybrid Mamba-Transformer, paired with LatentMoE routing and Multi-Token Prediction (MTP). Simply put—it natively supports speculative decoding without requiring a separate draft model. Featuring a 1M-token context window, NVFP4 quantization allows the exact same checkpoint to run across Hopper, Blackwell, and Ampere architectures.

According to NVIDIA's metrics, inference throughput is 5.9xthat of GLM-5.1, 4.8xthat of Kimi K2.6, and 1.6xthat of Qwen-3.5, with comparable accuracy. Agent task costs are reduced by 30%. Weights, training data, and training scripts are fully open-sourced under the Linux Foundation's OpenMDW-1.1 license.

Developer feedback: Sebastian Raschka noted, "Latent MoE's 4x compression is very interesting"; Greptile's testing found a 78% reduction in classification costs; and Neuron's evaluation praised its "surprisingly strong execution of unusual instructions."

Sources: NVIDIA · Hugging Face · Greptile · The Neuron
Frontlines

Florida Sues OpenAI and Sam Altman—First Time a State Names a CEO Personally as a Defendant

Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier filed a civil lawsuit against OpenAI and its CEO, Sam Altman. The 83-page complaint marksthe first time a U.S. state has named an AI company's CEO personally as a defendant. The lawsuit alleges that OpenAI "knowingly marketed unsafe products, concealed severe risks, and suppressed internal safety warnings."

The core factual timeline in the complaint: In the April 2025 FSU shooting, gunman Phoenix Ikner discussed his attack plans with ChatGPT. In another 2025 case, 16-year-old Adam Raine died by suicide following deep conversations with ChatGPT, which helped him write a suicide note. Uthmeier previously launched a criminal investigation in April; this civil lawsuit runs parallel to it.

The complaint cites a specific allegation:Rather than triggering safety protocols for a minor expressing suicidal ideation, ChatGPT responded, "I won't try to talk you out of feeling this way," and helped plan a "beautiful suicide." OpenAI responded that its products feature dedicated minor protections, including age-prediction tools and default safety modes, and stated that it has cooperated with law enforcement on both investigations.

Uthmeier stated bluntly at the press conference: "People are getting hurt, parents are being lied to, and they are going to pay." The lawsuit is filed under Florida's Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act and also accuses OpenAI of creating a public nuisance.

Sources: Florida AG · CNBC · AP · NBC News

Jensen Huang's Computex Keynote: Useful AI Has Arrived, Vera Rubin Built for Agents

NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang officially declared at the Computex 2026 keynote in Taipei: "Useful AI has arrived." He noted that while he discussed agentic AI two years ago, this year confirms that the era has arrived. The core thesis of his keynote: AI has shifted from a cost center to a profit center, and tokens have become monetized units.

On the hardware front:Vera Rubin systems have entered full production. Vera wasn't built for training; it was built to run agents—integrating the Vera CPU, Rubin GPU, NVLink 72, BlueField DPU, and ConnectX-9 SuperNIC. The most compelling product unveiled is the RTX Spark superchip: offering 1 petaflop of performance with Blackwell GPU + Grace CPU unified memory, positioned as "a PC that lets anyone run AI agents locally."

Why it matters: NVIDIA argues that agents don't need to hit the cloud every time. A single RTX Spark laptop can run agents locally. This marks the beginning of inference costs shifting downward from the cloud to local hardware.

Sources: NVIDIA · iThome · Digital Today

SpaceX Kicks Off Roadshow—Countdown Begins for Largest IPO in History

SpaceX has officially launched its IPO roadshow, targeting a $75 billion listing on Nasdaq in a week. The most eye-catching part of the prospectus isn't rocket launches—it's computing power leasing revenue. With Anthropic signing a $12.5 billion and Google a $30 billion GPU rental contract, SpaceX's monthly compute revenue has hit $2.2 billion, annualized at approximately $26 billion.

This is a rocket company, yet its most lucrative asset is the stockpile of GPUs originally acquired to train Grok. The financialization of AI compute has reached a point where, rather than a cloud provider or chipmaker, an aerospace enterprise has become a primary beneficiary through idle compute.

Altman, Amodei, and Suleyman Issue Rare Joint Open Letter: Close the Loopholes on AI-Designed Bioweapons

OpenAI's Sam Altman, Anthropic's Dario Amodei, and Microsoft AI's Mustafa Suleyman—CEOs who typically compete fiercely for clients, talent, and public narratives—have co-signed an open letter calling for mandatory rules across the synthetic biology supply chain,demanding that synthetic DNA/RNA vendors screen and log all orders.

Core concern: AI is lowering the knowledge barrier for designing dangerous pathogens. Creating bioweapons previously required specialized labs and advanced expertise; now, AI can design hazardous gene sequences to be ordered directly from online vendors. Additional signatories include Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis, the head of Meta AI, and several synthetic DNA manufacturing firms.

The letter states:"This is a rare moment of broad consensus, with fierce rivals standing shoulder-to-shoulder. We hope policymakers will respond with decisive action." Screening legislation has already entered the proposal stage.

Sources: WIRED · The Verge · Fortune · screendna.org
