ChatGPT's Memory System Completely Rewritten—Dreaming V3 Launches

OpenAI is rolling out Dreaming V3—a completely redesigned memory architecture—to Plus and Pro users in the U.S. This is not just a feature upgrade.The entire memory system has been rebuilt from scratch..

Previous memory systems functioned like sticky notes: you told ChatGPT what to remember, it noted it down, and deleting the chat erased it. Dreaming V3 is different—it actively builds connections across your multiple conversations. Whether you discussed a project, mentioned a preference, or asked about a certain type of question, it weaves these threads together to form a persistent understanding of you, eliminating the need to say "remember this" every time.

What is the cost?Deleting chats no longer equals erasing memory. Deep Wire reports that information extracted by the model from conversations enters a persistent layer; even if you delete a chat, what it learned remains. OpenAI states that users can manage memory via settings, but it is enabled by default.

What it means for you: Every time you chat with ChatGPT, you are training its memory system—not training the model, but training its understanding of "who you are." If you haven't changed your memory settings, Dreaming V3 is already doing this.

NVIDIA Open-Sources Cosmos 3—A Single Model That Reasons, Generates Worlds, and Executes Actions

NVIDIA has open-sourced Cosmos 3, a family of "omnimodal world models." Unlike previous video-generation-only versions, Cosmos 3 packs three capabilities into a single model:Physical Reasoning + World Generation + Action Generation. In other words, it understands how objects move, can generate scene visuals, and can output commands for robots to act.

Two versions are available:Cosmos3-Nano (16B, runnable on a single workstation GPU) and Cosmos3-Super (64B, for data centers). NVIDIA has also open-sourced checkpoints, training scripts, six synthetic data datasets, and an evaluation benchmark called HUE.Fully Open.

The goal of this technology: to eliminate the need for humans to painstakingly annotate data for robots, enabling AI to generate its own training data and train itself. NVIDIA states that Cosmos 3 has achieved open-source SOTA levels under the OpenMDW-1.1 license.

Sources: NVIDIA · All About AI

University of Cambridge Completes World's First Phase I Clinical Trial of an AI-Designed Vaccine—Safe and Effective

The University of Cambridge and biotech firm DIOSynVax announced that an AI-designed vaccine candidate has completed its Phase I clinical trials—— in 39 volunteers with no significant side effects. This is not a vaccine targeting a single virus—it targets the entire Sarbeco coronavirus subgenus, including SARS, COVID-19, and bat coronaviruses with future cross-species spillover potential.

Project lead Professor Jonathan Heeney noted that AI has shifted vaccine design from "reactive" to "proactive"—rather than waiting for a new virus to emerge, AI is employed from the outset to design an antigen covering an entire viral family. AI is not merely accelerating existing pipelines; it is redesigning the very shape of the antigen.

What it means for you: When the next pandemic hits, vaccine design timelines could shrink from years to months. AI isn't just running pipelines faster—it is redesigning the antigen itself to target a whole category of viruses rather than just one.

Google to Pay SpaceX $920M Monthly for GPUs—Just One Week Before SpaceX's Historic IPO

A regulatory filing reveals thatGoogle will pay SpaceX $920 million per month to lease approximately 110,000 NVIDIA GPUs from October 2026 to June 2029. At this rate, the contract is valued at approximately $30 billion. Google described this as "short-term bridge capacity" as demand for Gemini Enterprise far exceeds projections and internal infrastructure build-out lags behind.

This marks the second massive compute contract signed by SpaceX, following a similar $12.5 billion agreement previously secured by Anthropic. Combined, SpaceX will rake in $2.2 billion monthly in compute revenue—translating to an annualized run rate of about $26 billion. This revenue will be featured in the IPO prospectus ahead of SpaceX's Nasdaq listing next week, targeting a raise of $75 billion.

Core takeaway: Compute has transformed from a cost center into a revenue engine. xAI's Colossus cluster was originally built to train Grok, but Grok usage has declined. Rather than letting GPUs sit idle, leasing them to the companies most starved for compute makes financial sense. Google is also a long-term SpaceX investor, with its post-IPO stake projected to exceed $100 billion in value.

Sources: TNW · CNBC · SEC Filing

Meta built an AI feature that automatically generates news—and had to shut it down after it fabricated fake royal stories

Meta has confirmed it will shut down the "For You"ing feed within its standalone AI app. The feature automatically generates complete clickbait articles, with both text and images synthesized on the fly. The issue escalated when it fabricated outlandish content: a story about the British royal family was paired with an image featuring two Queen Elizabeth II figures image (the Queen passed away years ago), a completely fictional "first-person" story with entirely fake sources and bylines—and no AI label whatsoever.

Meta spokesperson Tracy Clayton called it a "limited test" and stated it had been pulled. However, journalists found that the feature had been rolled out to multiple users for at least several months. Meta has not explained:how a feature capable of fabricating fake news, forging expert bylines, and generating unlabeled synthetic images ever passed internal review.

What it means for you: Meta faces tightening regulations on synthetic media in the EU and UK. Pushing a fictional news generator at this juncture highlights that AI product release velocities are outstripping corporate safety protocols.

Sources: AI Chat Daily · The Verge

Microsoft Claims MAI Models Use "Clean Data"—While Its Own Paper Details 24.2 Billion Pages of Scraped Data

At the Build conference on June 2, Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman claimed on stage that MAI-Thinking-1's training data was "100% enterprise-grade, clean, and commercially licensed." Three days later, Microsoft's own research paper detailed on page 80 its training data pipeline—24.2 billion pages of Common Crawl data, a public web-scraping repository without licensing guarantees.

The discrepancy was uncovered by developer Simon Willison, who was in attendance at Build and read the paper after the keynote. Spotting the contradiction, he updated his blog with a blunt assessment:"These models share the exact same licensing issues as every other mainstream LLM."

Frankly, training on Common Crawl is not a violation—it is standard industry practice. The real issue lies in the gap between the keynote and the paper. Microsoft already had a compelling narrative—"We built a powerful model independently without relying on OpenAI"—but felt compelled to add "and the data is clean," only to be contradicted by its own documentation.

OpenAI Launches Lockdown Mode to Prevent Prompt Injection Data Exfiltration

OpenAI has released Lockdown Mode, a protection mode tailored for sensitive data. When enabled, ChatGPT disables real-time web browsing (restricting it to cached content), blocks pulling web images, and turns off Deep Research and Agent modes.Core objective: To prevent attackers from hiding malicious instructions within web pages that trick ChatGPT into exfiltrating your data.

Prompt injection is currently one of the hardest attack vectors to defend against—attackers embed hidden instructions in a webpage or file, and ChatGPT gets "hijacked" when reading it. Lockdown Mode doesn't try to be stronger; instead, itcuts off the entry points. The trade-off is reduced functionality. OpenAI puts it bluntly:"Lockdown Mode is not for everyone."

It is now rolling out to self-employed ChatGPT Business accounts and eligible personal accounts.What it means for you: If you use ChatGPT to process contracts, financial records, or client data—this feature is built for you.
