Midjourney Launches Full-Body Ultrasound CT: 358,000 Transducer Elements, Hailed as the First New Modality in 50 Years—Yet Lacks Clinical Validation

June 17 Midjourney released the Midjourney Scanner: a annular water-immersion full-body ultrasound CT.

Structurally, it consists of8960 sensors × 40 systems forming a ring, totaling 358,000 ultrasound transducer elements, with a diameter of 70 cm, generating approximately 17GB of data per second during scanning, about 40GB per body slice, requiring 21 servers for reconstruction. CEO David Holz called it"the first new whole-body medical imaging modality in 50 years.".

The current reality is modest: the team of about 9 people has only scanned about 12 individuals so far, with each scan taking 20 minutes (bottlenecked by bandwidth, algorithms, and DSP), and a claimed resolution of 0.5mm. The company also announced the opening of Midjourney Spa in San Francisco's Union Square—approximately 25000 square feet, four floors, housing 9-10 scanners, targeting 2027 opening by the end of the year. Discussions with the FDA have already begun.

The long-term vision is massive: targeting 50,000scanners and 1 billionscans per month. Holz stated that fewer than 12 machines running at full speed could scan the global volume of MRIs, and the cost per scan could be orders of magnitude cheaper than an MRI. However, rolling out thousands of Spas requires about $20 billionin upfront capital. The criticisms are equally sharp:No clinical validation, no sensitivity/specificity metrics..

Whether this machine can actually diagnose diseases is still far off, but it signifies that AI labs are starting to build "devices to feed body data to AI"—capturing the data pipeline right after models.

GLM-5.2 Passes the "Hands-On Test": Ranks 3rd on Knowledge Work Benchmarks at 1/13th the Cost of Fable—Not Achieved Through Benchmark Gaming

AINews marks June 19 as the breakthrough day for GLM-5.2. The community has always maintained a"benchmaxxed"skepticism toward open-source models. This time,authentic hands-on experience: Jeremy Howard(known for zero hype) stated it is "at least as good as Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5," with its only drawback being a lack of vision;Mat Velloso called it the first open-source model to pass his "daily driver" standard.

Artificial Analysis 's new benchmark AA-Briefcase provides hard data: simulating real knowledge work centered around multi-week projects and thousands of fragmented inputs. GLM-5.2 scored 1266 Elo, ranking behind Fable 5 (1587) and Opus 4.8 (1356), making it the strongest non-Anthropic model. Even more valuable is the economics: Fable 5 averages $31 per task, Opus 4.8 is $10.40, GPT-5.5 xhigh $3.68, while GLM-5.2 costs only $2.40—about 1/13.

Poolside Open-Sources 225B Coding Model Laguna M.1: Apache 2.0, 256K Context—Built for Long-Horizon Agent Coding, Not Benchmarks

French startup Poolside open-sourced Laguna M.1 weights under Apache 2.0 with a 256K context. Architecturally, it is a 70-layer sparse MoE: total parameters at 225B, active parameters per token at 23B, 256, top-k=16, explicitly optimized forlong-horizon agent coding.

Demo for local enthusiasts: 3-bit MLX quantization running on an M3 Max 128GB machine achieves about 26 tok/s, with peak memory around 100GB.

Within a week, three major open-source lines—GLM-5.2, Laguna M.1, and North Mini Code—emerged, transforming the open-source foundation for coding agents from "short on talent" to "overcrowded."

Robots Lack Data—Some People Specialize in Folding T-Shirts and Moving Boxes to Profit from AI Labs

Four MIT Youths Made Four Counterintuitive Decisions—Building the Fastest-Growing Software in SaaS History in Three Years

Michael Truell, 25, whose parents are both journalists. He won a $10,000 prize for a programming game at 15, and took an investor test at 18—finishing a one-hour test inten minutes, and after finishing it, he in turn posed a puzzle to the investors, which took them even longer to solve. He and three MIT classmatesAman Sanger, Sualeh Asif, Arvid Lunnemarkhe started a company in 2022, making four counterintuitive decisions:

First—Working backward from solutions to problems. Knowing what AI could transform programming into, they started from the destination and worked backward. Truell said: "This defies startup convention. But the AI field is full of new possibilities; perhaps we don't need to be constrained by niche problems and can start with a grand vision."

Second—Failing for a year before pursuing what they truly love. Feeling the programming space was too fiercely competitive (with Microsoft's Copilot and dozens of others fighting for it), they pivoted to automatic 3D CAD completion for mechanical engineering. It turned out that CAD data was orders of magnitude scarcer than code data, and they weren't mechanical engineers. After a few months, they abandoned it and returned to coding—rather than fleeing competition, face it head-on.

Third—Build an editor, not a plugin. Completely redesigning everything from the UI to the underlying architecture. Truell said: "If programming is going to change fundamentally, the interface must change." It's akin to saying: instead of painting within someone else's frame, I'll build my own frame.

Fourth—Ship the product before touching models. At the very beginning, they built the editor entirely by purchasing external APIs, facing the skepticism: "Aren't you leaving yourselves at the mercy of others by not mastering the core model?" His reply: Build the editor first and let more people use it; once there are enough users, the data will naturally follow.

All four decisions bet ontheir areas of deepest expertise. Going from zero to$4 billionin ARR within 24 months—a milestone no other B2B SaaS company has achieved. Subsequently, SpaceX acquired them for$60 billionin stock—Cursor needed compute to train its own models, and SpaceX holds 550,000 GPUs.Compute is the ultimate bargaining chip.

Sources: Sohu · Founded · CNBC · YC Interview
