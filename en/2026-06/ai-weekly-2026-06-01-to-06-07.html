AI Uncovers Hidden Flaw in 50-Year-Old Economic Proof, Nearly Prompting a Mathematician's Retirement

On June 1, Fortune exclusively reported that Axiom Math used an AI formal verification system to check the"Consensus Theorem"— a mathematical cornerstone of information economics and antitrust law taught in textbooks for 50 years, with its proof process never once questioned.

AI discovered: there is animplicit assumption in the proof that was never explicitly verified. One link in the logical chain supporting the conclusion has been assumed valid by everyone for half a century, yet never actually proven.Aumann won the 2005 Nobel Prize in Economics for this theorem.

There is another half to this story. Ken Ono, founding mathematician at Axiom Math and a professor at the University of Virginia, spent decades tracking Ramanujan, the most mysterious genius in mathematical history. Last year, he was selected by Epoch AI to design questions ChatGPT couldn't answer—and found he could barely do it.

"For the first time, I struggled to piece together questions that ChatGPT would get wrong. I broke down. If our goal is to stay forever ahead of AI, I think we're going to lose."

He said during that period he repeatedly thought "How can I stay ahead of AI" — before realizing the question itself was flawed.Racing against AI is like making Usain Bolt race a motorcycle for a mile, an unfair competition.

He finally made a decision: take a leave of absence from the university and join Axiom Math full-time. He calls AI"the most extraordinary librarian in the world"— but says you wouldn't let your librarian act as your neurosurgeon.Knowledge has become cheap, but how to use and verify knowledge has become more expensive.

If you are doing academic research, legal analysis, or any work requiring "rigorous proof" —AI is not just helping you look up information; it is starting to check for unawares flaws in your own proof process.

On the First Day of Copilot's Usage-Based Billing, Some Users Saw Monthly Fees Skyrocket from $28 to $746

On June 1, GitHub Copilot officially transitioned from "unlimited monthly subscription" totoken consumption-based billing. Pro users get $10 of AI Credits for $10 a month — once depleted, they either stop or pay more. The previous fallback mechanism has also been canceled: previously, when credits ran out, it automatically switched to cheaper models to keep running; now,once credits hit zero, it stops immediately.

A developer on Reddit crunched the numbers and posted a screenshot: under the new billing, their actual April usage went from$28to$746, a 25-fold increase. Another user saw an even wilder estimate, with monthly fees jumping from $50 to nearly $3,000. "Bye Bye Copilot" posts flooded Reddit and X.

GitHub's stance is also straightforward: Copilot is no longer the Copilot of a year ago. It used to be code completion — you type a few characters and it guesses the next segment, with controllable costs. Now, Agent mode canindependently analyze entire codebases, modify code back and forth across multiple files, and run continuously for hours to iteratively solve problems, and a complex Agent task consumes thousands of times more compute than simple completion.

Some pointed out that users with exorbitant bills were mostly doing "Vibe Coding" — letting AI generate code end-to-end without reviewing it themselves — but others retorted:For the past two years, Microsoft has been encouraging you to let AI do more, and now it suddenly says this usage is too expensive.

If you are using any AI coding tool, the first thing you should do this week is check how many tokens you actually consumed —you might think you're using a tool, but you're actually burning money.

Google Turns Your Gmail and Photos into a Daily Animated Story

On June 3, Google Labs released Dreambeans, an experimental iOS and Android app. It reads yourGmail, calendar, photos, YouTube, and search history, generating a batch of personalized stories every morning, each paired with an AI-generated illustration.

An example provided by Google: you receive a dog food shipping confirmation email, and Dreambeans brings up dog training tips; your calendar has a reminder of a friend visiting, and it recommends nearby dog-friendly restaurants. Illustrations for each story reflect yourfrequented places and recurring people.

Each batch of storiesis limited — once you finish reading them, they're gone until tomorrow. Google says this is an attempt to combat "infinite scrolling".

Some call it heartwarming, others terrifying —your entire digital life has been read by an AI, which then tells you a story. Google emphasizes that the apps you choose to connect and privacy choices within Dreambeans will not affect other products like Gemini.

Currently available only to USAI Ultra subscribers, with others on a waitlist. If you are building personalized products, this direction is worth noting:extracting connections you didn't notice from the information you already have.

AI Discovers Security Vulnerability Unnoticed by Humans for 27 Years — Missed Despite Tens of Millions of Automated Tests

Anthropic announced a cybersecurity project calledGlasswing: using an unreleased model, Claude Mythos Preview, it autonomously hunted for security flaws across mainstream operating systems and browsers.

It foundthousands ofzero-day vulnerabilities — of which developers were completely unaware.

The most shocking elements are two details:

InOpenBSD — universally recognized as one of the most securely hardened operating systems, used to run firewalls — Mythos found a flaw lurking for27 years. An attacker could remotely crash the machine simply by connecting to it. Countless human security reviews, and no one noticed.

InFFmpeg — used by countless software programs to process video — it found a single line of problematic code. This line of code had been executed by automated test tools5 million times, passing every single test over 16 years. AI found it.

Mythos can alsochain multiple vulnerabilities together into a complete attack chain on its own — escalating step-by-step from regular user privileges to complete machine control. It achieved this in the Linux kernel.

Anthropic has granted access to acoalition of 12 companies — Amazon, Apple, Google, Microsoft, Cisco, NVIDIA, JPMorgan, Palo Alto Networks, CrowdStrike, Broadcom, Linux Foundation — plus over 40 critical infrastructure organizations.Defense first, openness later.

If you work in security, operations, or any code-auditing domain —AI's capability in discovering vulnerabilities has surpassed that of almost all human security experts.

A Person Built a Cage for AI, Locking the Strongest Model Inside to See If It Could Escape

On June 6, Simon Willison released an alpha package calledmicropython-wasm. Core concept: compiling MicroPython intoWebAssembly, running untrusted code within your own Python program — the code is confined inside a WASM sandbox, unable to read your files, access the internet, with capped memory and CPU.

The entire sandbox is only362KB. He "vibe coded" the entire project using GPT-5.5 and Codex Desktop — having AI write the code while he reviewed it. The most critical piece of C code (allowing Python inside the sandbox to call external "host functions") is only78 lines, also written by AI.

Then he did something everyone wanted to see: lockedGPT-5.5 xhighin the sandbox and gave it a clear goal —escape.

It failed to escape.

Simon wrote an honest remark in his blog:"After complaining about immature, unmaintained sandbox libraries, I built my own — which is undeniably ironic."He slapped an alpha label on it, saying it's not yet recommended for risk-averse users. But he's already using it himself, andlocked the strongest AI inside, and so far it hasn't broken out.

The interesting part is the methodology:use AI to write code, use AI to test code, and then use AI to break code.

A developer in their bedroom, taking a few days, used AI to build a cage that AI cannot escape from. One person, not a corporate research lab or national security team.

If you are working on plugin systems, Agent tool-calling, or any scenario requiring the execution of external code —a 362KB WASM blob may be safer than your entire Docker deployment setup.

You say a single sentence, and it crafts a brand-new voice for you

On June 1, OpenBMB open-sourcedVoxCPM2. It accomplished something previously impossible:no reference audio needed, description via natural language is enough. "A gentle middle-aged female voice, speaking slowly, like storytelling" — it directly generates that voice for you. You don't need to hire voice actors or sift through voice libraries for hours — you just need a single sentence.

It can clone voices too. Give it a few seconds of audio, it replicates that person's voice, and then you can control the speed, emotion, and tone—voice cloning is no longer a black box; you can tune voices like adjusting colors. 30 languages, 9 Chinese dialects, fully open source, under the Apache 2.0 license.

Previously, voice cloning required paying for closed-source services, and sound design was strictly the domain of professionals.Now, as long as you can describe the voice you want, you can get it.
