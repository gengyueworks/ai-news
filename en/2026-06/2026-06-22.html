Zuckerberg Spends $14.3 Billion on AI—While His Own Engineers Still Use Claude

Zuckerberg Invests$14.3 BillionAcquiring nearly half of Scale AI and bringing in 28-year-old Alexandr Wang as Meta's Chief AI Officer, followed by the launch of Meta's first proprietary frontier modelMuse Spark.

The result? In coding task tests, Meta's internal engineersstill prefer Anthropic's Claude. Wang himself admitted that Muse Spark lags behind competitors in coding. While visual understanding received praise, a developer's core scenario remains writing code.

Even more awkwardly, insiders point out that Muse Sparkrelies on Llama 4's code and datasets, despite Wang's previous claims that it was "built from scratch." Some insiders compared parts of the system to DeepSeek's latest model, suggesting a lack of innovation.

Why abandon open source? Wang provided a reason on Bloomberg Tech: internal testing revealed the model triggered "high-risk domains," particularlybiological risks, making an open-source release unviable. Consequently, the frontier is locked within proprietary products, restricted to Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, and Ray-Ban glasses. The opening of private APIs was described by the Financial Times as "limited."

Neurometric CEO Rob May put it bluntly: "The AI community is basically ignoring Meta right now."Muse Spark elicited a "yawn" from the community—the technology is not widespread, and developers cannot access it. Meta's stock has fallen 18% over the past 12 months, with 97.6% of revenue derived from ads, leaving its AI transition at risk of becoming an "expensive detour."

Apertus Mini Launches: 16 Small Models from 0.5B to 4B—The Swiss Say "Open Source" Means You Can Actually Rebuild It

The Swiss AI Initiative (EPFL + ETH Zurich + CSCS) has releasedApertus Mini——16distilled and quantized open-source small models ranging from 0.5B to 4B parameters. Unlike "open weights"—it fully discloses training data, processing code, and complete training recipes. You can rebuild it, audit it, and build the next generation upon it.

In terms of large models, there areApertus 8BandApertus 70B. The 8B quantized version requires only 6GB of VRAM, running on standard PCs. The 70B covers1000+ languages, with multilingual capabilities built in from day one.

What's most unique is thatEU AI Act compliance is built in from the start—training data comes with provenance documentation, PII features a hashing filtration system (with SNAI periodically publishing hashes of deletion requests), and memorization prevention technology is included. Using this model in a regulated industry gives you actual artifacts to show regulators during audits.

With 236 points and 83 comments on Hacker News, one commenter wrote:"They empower the community to create the next generation of SOTA models—the only way LLMs truly become sovereign AI."

310 People, 12 Hours, A Coffee-Queue Meeting—The Top Three Winners of the Anthropic Hackathon Showcase Human Creativity

June 13, San Francisco.1,500applied,310attended, each receiving $500 in credits, with one day to turn ideas into working projects. They used Claude Opus 4.8, released just two weeks prior.

1st Place: Tekton—by Holly Tang and Austin Burgess. They 3D-reconstructed Tang dynasty architecture and Notre-Dame spires, with every component traceable to historical documents. Opus 4.8's independent sub-agents validated each reconstruction in an isolated context, running a self-correction loop for 20 rounds until passing. The two met a month ago while waiting in a coffee line at a Code with Claude event. Holly said:"Whenever I watch documentaries and see beautiful architecture destroyed by fire, it always makes me sad."

2nd Place: Sim Francisco—by Tanmayi Priya Dasari and Tejas Prabhune. Using Census data, they built10,000synthetic San Francisco residents to simulate citywide public decision-making with an error rate of just 0.4%. Opus 4.8 wrote its own clustering algorithm to compress 10,000 people into 300 representatives—reducing inference costs by 10x to 100x. The pair met in the Berkeley ML club.

3rd Place: Custom Universe—by Jake Stevens and Mauricio Pereira. They turned a smartphone photo into a real-time editable 3D scene to generate synthetic training data for robotics labs—scanning factory equipment, dropping it into the scene, and generating data to fine-tune robot models. Jake and Mauricio met on-site at the event.

Three teams. Three completely different projects. Three human connections—a coffee queue, a university club, and an on-the-spot meeting.AI provided the tools, humans made the choices.
