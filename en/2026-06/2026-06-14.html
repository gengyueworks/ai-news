Fable 5 has arrived, and then it was blocked
Fable 5 has arrived, and then it was blocked

Anthropic has released its most powerful model to date Fable 5 and Mythos 5, slashing the price in half.

Just as many were getting ready to test it, the U.S. government issued export controls two days later—requiring the suspension of Fable 5 access in specific regions.

A deeper narrative underlies this development. The Wall Street Journal and The Verge broke the news first:Amazon CEO Andy Jassy met with government officials before the order was issued.

Amazon's security team reportedly provided the government with a safety assessment of Fable 5, which is said to have triggered the move.

This is a moment that makes you stop and reflect.This marks the first time a frontier model has been formally subjected to export controls.

Furthermore, it is not merely a bureaucratic directive, but driven by cloud platform security assessments. Model accessibility is no longer just a question of "where the API switch is located."

Sources: Anthropic · The Verge | The Wall Street Journal
GLM 5.2 Climbs to 316 Upvotes on Hacker News
GLM 5.2 Climbs to 316 Upvotes on Hacker News

Meanwhile, GLM 5.2 surged to 316 upvotes on Hacker News, surpassing the overseas popularity records of many domestic models.

This is one of the best achievements ever recorded by a Chinese community model on the HN homepage.

Landing on the homepage on the exact same day as the Fable 5 export controls—discussing technological advancement and geopolitical regulation side by sideis a footnote to the week in its own right.

Sources: Hacker News
Anthropic Asked 52,000 People What They Want AI to Do
Anthropic Asked 52,000 People What They Want AI to Do

Anthropic asked 52,000 people what they want AI to do: 48% said curing diseases, 36% said helping people with disabilities, and 64% expressed concerns about losing their jobs.

They also launched the Claude Corps Fellowship, empowering young people to bring AI into their local communities.

Taken together, these two developments resemble an industry blueprint. Model makers are shifting their narrative:from "how powerful my model is" to "what real problems my model solves for whom".

Public expectations and fears coexist; turning a blind eye to either is no longer an option.

Sources: Anthropic · anthropic.com
