269-Page Federal AI Framework Draft Dropped—3-Year Preemption, Superseding All 50 State Laws

The U.S. federal level has seen the most comprehensive AI regulatory framework draft in history."Great American AI Act"(Great American AI Act) discussion draft—co-drafted by a bipartisan team, 269 pages spanning 12 chapters.

The core mechanism is straightforward:Federal preemption for the first 3 years. The bill requires large AI developers with over $500 million in annual revenue to publish "frontier AI frameworks" disclosing whether their models pose "catastrophic risks"—defined as foreseeable material risks resulting in over 50 deaths/injuries or over $1 billion in property damage. Frameworks and updates must be published annually.

Who investigates? The bill establishes under NIST the CAISI(Center for AI Standards and Innovation) into law, authorizing a 3-year, $300 million budget to hire "critical technical experts" above government salary caps. CAISI certifies Independent Validation Organizations (IVOs), funded by major developers, to audit every six months. Audit results are reported to CAISI and can be referred to the Department of Justice for civil litigation.Failed audit? The Attorney General can sue you. State attorneys general can also join.

Safety incidents must be reported within 15 days, and imminent risks within 24 hours. The bill also protects whistleblowers. CAISI's own authorization is also for 3 years, terminating upon expiration.

Why it matters to you: This directly overrides Colorado's state AI law taking effect on June 30. If passed at the federal level, AI development regulations across all 50 states will be unified within 3 years—provided it successfully completes the legislative process.

European Data Protection Board Rules: ChatGPT's Persistent Memory Constitutes "Profiling"—GDPR Obligations Take Effect Immediately

The day after OpenAI launched Dreaming V3,the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) released a preliminary opinion: the persistent memory of AI systems constitutes profiling under the GDPR. This is not a future obligation—requirements like user consent, right to deletion, and data minimization apply right now, waiting for no one.

The EDPB's stance is clear: a system that automatically reads a user's multi-year chat history, continuously synthesizes and updates personal profiles, and retains the memory layer even after users delete conversations is no longer a "convenience feature," but is engaging in behavioral profiling. GDPR regulations on profiling apply from day one: Is there a legal basis? Is it transparent? Can users truly delete their data?

Before releasing Dreaming V3, OpenAI invited the EFF and Berkman Klein Center for a privacy review. The EFF's preliminary assessment was that "the level of privacy engineering is significantly higher than typical consumer AI"—but that's relative, not a free pass.The GDPR standard is not "better than peers," but "compliant."

Yet OpenAI itself admits: the memory summary page does not necessarily contain everything the system remembers. Deleting a single chat does not remove the memories extracted from it. The audit trail actually narrows. This creates a fundamental contradiction with the EDPB's requirement that users must be able to "truly delete" their data.

Microsoft AI Head: Contract with OpenAI Renegotiated Six Months Ago—We Were "Unleashed" to Pursue AGI

VentureBeat interviewed at Build 2026 Mustafa Suleyman, CEO of Microsoft AI. In the interview, he disclosed a key piece of information:About six months ago, Microsoft renegotiated its contract with OpenAI, lifting several clauses that previously restricted Microsoft from independently developing AI.

Specific restrictions included: Microsoft was explicitly barred from training large models on its own, with the contract even setting scale caps calculated in FLOPS. After modifying the contract, these restrictions were removed, and Suleyman immediately formed the MAI Superintelligence Team, releasing 7 proprietary models at Build spanning reasoning, code, images, speech, and other modalities.

Suleyman's exact words: "We were essentially 'unleashed' from our contract with OpenAI about six months ago, officially allowing us to pursue superintelligence. So it's still early days."—Yet launching seven models simultaneously indicates preparation began long ago. OpenAI remains Microsoft's partner and model provider, but Microsoft is no longer just a "model buyer."

Google Launches Colab CLI—Direct GPU Access from the Terminal, Agent-Ready

Google has released the Colab CLI, bridging local terminals and remote Colab runtimes. You can now use colab --gpu A100 to instantly provision a high-performance GPU environment, run Python scripts locally, and pull results back using colab download.

Colab CLI is open-source under Apache 2.0. It includes a built-in agent skill file(COLAB_SKILL.md), allowing any terminal-capable AI agent (Claude Code, Codex, AutoGPT, etc.) to directly read the file and learn how to use Colab CLI. Google's example: having an agent fine-tune Gemma 3 1B on Colab using QLoRA—fully automated.

Developers' immediate reaction was straightforward: Fedir Martynov said, "Calling T4 GPUs from the terminal is the right direction, but let's hope authentication and quotas don't turn into a browser verification loop—that will kill Agents."

Sources: Google Developers Blog · InfoQ · Help Net Security · MarkTechPost
Perplexity Builds "Hybrid Inference Scheduler"—Sensitive Data Computed Locally, Other Tasks Sent to Cloud

Perplexity announced at Computex 2026 its hybrid local-server inference scheduler, reportedly the industry's first. The system automatically determines where a task should be executed: tasks involving sensitive information like financial records and health data remain on local devices for computation; tasks requiring massive reasoning capabilities are automatically routed to the cloud.

This system is model-agnostic and chip-agnostic—confirmed to run on Intel Core Ultra Series 3 and NVIDIA RTX Spark. It launches on Perplexity Computer in July. Perplexity Personal Computer (local version) is already running on Mac, with the Windows version open for waitlist.
