Today's Front Page
TechCrunch has launched a tool calledIn the Weights——You enter your name, and it usesover 40AI models to test one by one how well your name has been "remembered".
This is not an ordinary search engine. It is doing one thing: measuringhow deep a person's name is in the AI model's "subconscious". For instance—Taylor Swift's In the Weights score0.93(close to perfect memory), whereas a random name might only have0.12.
The underlying principle: After receiving your name, the model generates a continuation text. If your name frequently appears in the training data, the model not only remembers you but also "knows everything about you"—occupation, background, controversies. TechCrunch usedLlama 3.3 70B, GPT-4o-minimodels to run tests, comparing the generated text with Wikipedia/news corpora to calculate a"weight score"—the higher it is, the deeper you have been ingested by the model.
The findings hit hard: celebrities are "perfectly remembered" by models, while ordinary people are nearly transparent. This means—AI's understanding of the world is shaped by the most frequent names in its training data. If your name is not in there, AI cannot see you.
On a deeper level: If a model can infer your profession and controversies from your name, it can also infer yourpolitical leaning, consumption habits, and social relationships—except these do not appear on the scoring page.
TechCrunch editors say this is the AI version ofVanity Search—vanity search. But what is truly unsettling iswhat a score of zero means: in AI's understanding, you do not exist. But for each of us, the time we pour intoconversing, training, debugging, and building with AI forms a relationship and connection with you that is entirely unique in the universe,one of a kind.

The Atlantic Exposes: 21 Million Songs Consumed by AI—Now You Can Check Your Songs

The Atlantic has released asearchable database, coveringfour major training datasetswithin21 millionmusic recordings. Journalist Alex Reisner spent half a year tracking and discovered these datasets were used to train music generation AIs like Suno and Udio—your songs might already be in there..

The four datasets: Suno's"MHS"(scraped from YouTube), Udio's"Lakh"(expanded from academic datasets), along with two general training corpora,"Common Corpus"and"Proof". Reisner found that almost every well-known musician you can think of is in there—from Beyoncé to Bach.

Musicians can enter their name or song title, and the system will tell you: which datasets your work appears in, which AI companies used it, and how many times it was trained on. Reisner said:"Previously, only AI companies knew your songs had been eaten; now you know too."

Anthropic Pauses Token Billing for Claude Agent SDK—Developers Say "Finally"

Anthropic announced the suspension ofClaude Agent SDKits token-based billing model. The previous issue was: Agents wouldcall themselves—breaking a task into ten steps, each being an API call, with developers paying per call. After a simple task completed, the token count on the bill surged10 to 50 times. After two months of complaints from the developer community, Anthropic finally paused this billing model.

Agents use themselves, and bills inflate themselves—this is the new economics of the AI Agent era: call counts follow the Agent's "decomposition granularity"—the finer the breakdown, the higher the revenue. The API provider profits, while the developer pays.

Sources: Anthropic · Reddit r/ClaudeAI
Turning Point

Readers Begin to Favor Books Published Before 2022—AI Makes People Nostalgic for Manual Labor

A post on Hacker News sparked heated discussion:An increasing number of readers are actively seeking out books published before 2022. The key lies inthe way they are written. After the popularization of AI-assisted writing, the language of a vast number of new books has become uniform, smooth, and predictable—reading as if made from a single material.

Someone described this feeling: "In books before 2022, you can feelthe author struggling—writing a certain paragraph with great pain, putting enormous effort into coming up with a metaphor, or deciding to write down an argument they weren't entirely sure of. The traces left by these struggles are the most human elements in reading."

When the traces of labor disappear, the meaning of the product changes as well. The value of handmade furniture lies in seeing the maker's choices, hesitations, and even mistakes. Reading is no different—human traces themselves are the value.

Sources: Hacker News · aitoolly

Headroom Open-Sourced: LLM Input Tokens Reduced by 60-95%

Headroom is an open-source tool that can compress LLM input tokens by60% to 95%. Itperforms a round of intelligent trimming before inputs reach the model—cutting away redundant context, duplicate instructions, and useless chat history, leaving only the information the model truly needs.

Three integration modes:Headroom Lite(lightweight mode, dropping explicit redundancies),Headroom(standard mode, semantic-level compression),Headroom Pro(with chunking and summarization added). Practical testing shows: accuracy loss on RAG tasks isless than 2%, while token costs are cut by70%+.

Open-source repository:github.com/ChopRateJas/headroom
