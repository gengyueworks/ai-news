The Verge / Wired · June 29
Vibe coding's security bill has arrived: 5000 public applications lack authentication

On June 29, The Verge cited Wired and Red Access research showing that approximately 5000 public applications built with popular vibe-coding tools lack authentication mechanisms, with nearly 2000 of them suspected of leaking sensitive data.The biggest issue isn't erroneous code, but rather "running a local demo and deploying it straight to the cloud"—ignoring configurations, leaving databases unlocked, and omitting authentication.

  • AI tools telling you it's "secure" doesn't mean it actually is
  • The more widespread vibe coding becomes, the risks don't grow linearly; they explode alongside the number of applications

Anthropic New Economic Index: Claude Is No Longer Just Chatting, It's Producing Artifacts

A year ago, most usage resembled "conversation." Now, following the growth of Claude Code and Cowork, usage increasingly resembles long-running agentic tasks—shifting AI's unit from a "single response" to a "block of tasks."
