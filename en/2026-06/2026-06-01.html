Today's Front Page

Anthropic Files Confidentially for IPO—Valuation Nears $1 Trillion

On June 1, Anthropic confidentially submitted its S-1 registration statement, officially initiating its IPO process. Following its recent $65 billion funding round, the company's valuation has reached $965 billion. This puts Anthropic on track to go public this autumn, setting up a direct showdown with OpenAI over who hits the public markets first and commands a higher valuation.

Background: Over the past year, Anthropic's valuation skyrocketed from $380 billion (February) to $965 billion (May), trailing only OpenAI. Alongside SpaceX's $2 trillion target, this year's IPO market is absorbing the public debuts of two AI giants simultaneously.

Anthropic's S-1 filing will reveal the company's financial data for the first time—giving the outside world a clear look at whether AI lab business models are actually profitable.

On the same day, Anthropic announced that Project Glasswing would expand into critical infrastructure—though that expansion was officially unveiled on June 2.IPO ahead, safety capability showcase following. This is no coincidence.

Frontier

NVIDIA Cosmos 3 Released: The First Fully Open Physical AI World Model—Reasoning, Generating, Acting

Jensen Huang unveiled at GTC / Computex Taipei NVIDIA Cosmos 3, hailed as the world's first fully open omnimodel. It simultaneously understands text, images, video, ambient sound, and physical actions—taking first place across all physical AI benchmarks.

Two versions:Cosmos 3 Nano (16B parameters, for workstations, real-time inference) and Cosmos 3 Super (64B parameters, for data centers). Fully open source: model weights, training scripts, datasets, and deployment tools are all public. NVIDIA also launched the Cosmos Coalition—a cross-company collaborative organization whose members include Agile Robots, Black Forest Labs, Runway, Skild AI, among others.

Cosmos 3 does more than chat—it perceives the physical world. Robotics, autonomous driving, and warehouse management, previously stitched together from disparate tech stacks, can now be handled by a single model.

Sources: NVIDIA · GamesBeat · Tech Startups

NVIDIA DGX Station for Windows—An AI Supercomputer Running 1-Trillion-Parameter Models on Your Desk

Also announced at GTC Taipei was DGX Station for Windows, the world's first desktop-grade AI supercomputer. Featuring 20 petaflops of FP4 performance, 748GB of memory, and local execution of up to 1-trillion-parameter models, or running hundreds of parallel AI agents simultaneously.

Jointly developed by NVIDIA and Microsoft, this machine runs OpenShell (NVIDIA's open-source agent runtime), integrates Microsoft's MXC secure container technology, and is slated for a Q4 release. Jensen Huang called it "the first complete redesign of the PC in 40 years," while Microsoft partners stated it "unlocks an entirely new tier of AI performance on Windows."

Sources: SiliconANGLE · Nvidia Blog
Pivot

Alphabet's $80 billion Equity Offering—AI Arms Race Enters "Infinite Ammo" Mode

Alphabet announced it is raising $80 billion, with Berkshire Hathaway subscribing to $10 billion on its own. This is the largest capital raise in Alphabet's history, earmarked explicitly for expanding AI infrastructure and compute capacity. After raising its capital expenditure forecast to $180–$190 billion just this April, the company is now directly financing further acceleration.

Alphabet stated in its announcement:"The company is experiencing robust demand from enterprises and consumers for AI solutions and services, at levels that exceed our current supply capabilities." When asked what keeps Google executives up at night, CEO Sundar Pichai gave a one-word answer:Compute.

Berkshire's participation is notable: when Buffett invested in Apple, he defined it as a "consumer brand," not a tech stock. This $10 billion investment in Alphabet—bringing its total stake to $20 billion—is Berkshire's most definitive endorsement of AI infrastructure yet. Greg Abel (Berkshire's new CEO) is signaling his investment thesis: a willingness to deploy massive capital into AI compute.

DGX Station for Windows—Desktop AI Supercomputer, Running Trillion-Parameter Models Locally

Today's biggest "You can do that?" moment comes from the NVIDIA-Microsoft collaboration: DGX Station for Windows. With 20 petaflops of FP4 and 748GB of memory, it means you can run a 1-trillion-parameter models right at your desk, or run hundreds of AI agents simultaneously.

This is not a concept machine; it is slated for a Q4 release. NVIDIA runs its OpenShell open-source agent runtime on it, with Microsoft handling MXC secure container integration. In Jensen Huang's own words: "The first complete redesign of the PC in 40 years."

Previously, developing with frontier models meant either waiting in cloud GPU queues or accepting that local execution was impossible. Now, a desktop-ready machine puts the power of choice back into the hands of developers.
