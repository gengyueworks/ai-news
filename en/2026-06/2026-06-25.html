OpenAI's custom inference chip Jalapeño: 9-month tapeout, ~10 PFLOPS—blueprints unreleased, entirely reverse-engineered by the community

On June 24, OpenAI and Broadcom announced Jalapeño—OpenAI's first custom chip tailored for LLM inference, powering ChatGPT, Codex, API, and future agent products. OpenAI emphasized performance-per-watt,with community reverse-engineering estimating it resembles a TPU: a die approaching reticle limits, approx. 216GB HBM3E, bandwidth around 7.1~7.4 TB/s, and roughly 10 PFLOPS under FP4. These figures are unconfirmed officially.

The true signal is the timeline:taking only 9 months from design to tapeout, which is abnormally fast for high-performance ASICs, with OpenAI hinting it used its own models to accelerate chip design. On the same day, Chris Lattner announced Qualcomm's acquisition of Modular (with Mojo's open-source plan unchanged).

With OpenAI building its own chips and Qualcomm absorbing compilers, the inference layer is shifting from "buying cards" to "self-developed full-stack"—NVIDIA's pricing sheet is no longer the only option.
